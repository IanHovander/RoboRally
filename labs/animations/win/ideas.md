# Win: 50 animation ideas (Lumen)

The moment: Maya (red Pebble) plays Move 2 from (2,1), lands on flag 2 at (4,1) and wins. Theo (blue Prism, at 5,3,
facing west toward flag 1) and Jun (yellow Cube, at 1,0, facing south toward the room) are still racing. Budget 6 s
including the move. Every idea ends with Maya at (4,1) facing east, Theo and Jun on their own squares facing the way
they faced, the board whole and the camera home.

## Banned (too obvious)

- Confetti or a particle burst in her colour
- Fireworks over the board
- A "WINNER" / "MAYA WINS" banner or any big headline text
- The winner spinning, hopping or bouncing on the spot
- The camera orbiting 360° around the winner
- Camera shake with a white flash
- Room goes dark and a spotlight or pillar lands on the winner (today's dim + red column)
- Expanding shockwave rings of light (today's rings)
- A trophy, crown, medal or podium appearing
- The losers drooping, slumping or turning grey
- A slow-motion replay of the final step
- A generic glow or bloom flare on the robot

## Ideas

1. **Long exposure.** As Maya touches flag 2, every trail drawn this game relights on the glass at once, each in its player's colour: her clean red line from the dock to flag 1 to here, with Theo's and Jun's wanderings beside it, the whole race as one photograph that fades back to smoked glass over three seconds. *Twist:* Lumen's trail, which forgets everything in a second, turns out to have remembered the whole game, and the room gets to laugh at Theo's loop-the-loops. It looks different every game.

2. **The reflection celebrates.** The camera sinks low to the glass, where Maya stands still and dignified while her reflection in the smoked glass bounces, wiggles and throws itself around wildly. As the camera rises home, the reflection snaps back into line a beat too late. *Twist:* the composed winner and her inner self in one frame. Reflection is the most Lumen material there is, and nobody expects it to have a mind of its own (a mirrored, dimmed clone of her model under the glass, puppeted separately).

3. **Theo makes the rainbow.** A thin thread of white light leaves flag 2's column sideways, crosses the glass and passes through Theo's Prism, which fans it into a full spectrum across the dark room; the colours sweep slowly, fold back into white and retract into the column. *Twist:* the rival makes the winner's celebration beautiful without meaning to. It's the only rainbow Lumen ever shows, and it only happens when a Prism is on the board, so it's a rare treat.

4. **Chladni figure.** Her landing strikes the slab like a glass plate: the board hums, and motes of light scattered over the glass shiver and slide into a standing-wave figure that settles into the unmistakable outline of a Pebble, while Theo and Jun tremble faintly on their squares. *Twist:* real physics (sand on a vibrating plate) draws the winner's portrait, and the figure follows the winner's form. The motes fade and the robots go still.

5. **Breath on the glass.** Maya exhales and a soft circle of fog blooms on the glass in front of her, catching the light; an unseen fingertip draws a little smiley in it with a squeaky chime, then the fog evaporates from the edges inward. *Twist:* the game's biggest moment is the most human, intimate thing you can do to a cold pane of glass: small and warm instead of loud.

6. **Ink in the glass.** A drop of her red falls into the slab at her feet and blooms inside the glass like ink in water, slow curling plumes drifting under Theo and Jun until the whole board glows red from within, then thinning and clearing back to smoked glass. *Twist:* the colour moves inside the material, in depth, with fluid curls, instead of a flat colour wash. For a moment the glass is a tank of water.

7. **She becomes the lamp.** Lumen's one warm key light slides down out of the dark and settles in Maya's eye, so every shadow on the board swings round to radiate away from her and Theo's and Jun's shadows stretch long across the glass; after a breath the light floats back up to its place. *Twist:* "one light" is the rule of the look, and the winner is handed it. You read the win in the losers' shadows.

8. **Glassblowing.** Maya's body swells like a gather of molten glass on a pipe, glowing orange-hot from inside and wobbling bigger and rounder until it surely must pop, then cools with a crackle of light and shrinks back to her polished red Pebble. *Twist:* the look's own craft. The near-pop is the joke, and the cooling from hot orange back to her red is the beauty.

9. **Kintsugi.** With a crisp crack, fractures shoot across the glass from her square as if the win were too heavy for the slab, and they fill with her colour like gold seams; then the seams knit shut from the tips inward and the board is whole again. *Twist:* the look's one material breaks on purpose and mends itself. The crack stops a hair from Theo, who flinches.

10. **The column pours into her.** Instead of flashing, flag 2's column of light drains slowly downward into Maya like liquid into a vessel, and she fills from the bottom up until she glows from inside; then the light rises back out of her and stands again as the column, now in her colour. *Twist:* "taking" the checkpoint, literally. The light is a liquid and the robot is the glass.

11. **Lighthouse.** Flag 2's column tips its light into a horizontal beam that sweeps the dark room like a lighthouse, over Jun (who freezes, caught), over Theo, and finally straight into the camera, washing the TV with a soft glare, before it stands upright again. *Twist:* the beam sweeps across the living room too, so everyone on the couch is lit for a moment.

12. **The loupe.** A photographer's loupe, a heavy ring of glass, slides across the board magnifying whatever it passes (the grid swells, Jun looms huge for an instant) and comes to rest over Maya, who fills the lens. *Twist:* Lumen is a light table, and this is what light tables are for. The close-up is made with refraction instead of a camera zoom (fake it by scaling what's under the ring).

13. **The stall.** Maya moves one space and stops dead at (3,1), one short of the flag; a beat, the room groans, and then she nudges the last space onto the flag almost apologetically. *Twist:* comic timing inside the rules (Move 2 is still two spaces, she just takes a breath between them), and the fake-out makes the celebration land twice as hard.

14. **Theo didn't get the memo.** While the column blazes, Theo, still heading west for flag 1, trundles one more space, stops, turns his eye slowly toward the light, and backs up onto his square with his trail retracing exactly. *Twist:* the rule "the game ends" acted out by one robot finding out late. His westward heading is already in the scenario, so it feels like his own idea.

15. **Jun's double take.** Jun has been facing south, toward the room, the whole time: he turns to look at Maya, turns back to the room, then whips round again at double speed and finally settles back facing the couch. *Twist:* the robot facing the couch reacts like the couch would, as if asking "did you see that?". He ends facing south as the rules have him.

16. **Jun rolls himself.** Jun, being a Cube, tips over face by face like a die, twice, and pips of light come up on the top face: a 2, the very Move 2 he needed; he tumbles back home and the pips fade. *Twist:* the Cube remembers it is a board-game die and tries to roll its own winning card, too late.

17. **Gravity forgets the losers.** With the race over, Theo and Jun drift up off the glass like let-go balloons, turning slowly and blinking, until a chime "remembers" them and they drop back onto their exact squares with a small glass bonk. *Twist:* the game no longer needs them, so physics stops bothering. The bonk gets the laugh.

18. **Sore loser's table flip.** Theo's side of the slab lurches up as if someone grabbed the table to flip it, everything tilts and Maya and Jun slide a few centimetres, then the heavy glass sinks back level and everyone slides exactly home. *Twist:* the board-game rage-quit, attempted on a calm glass slab that refuses to play along. It ends level, with every robot back on its square.

19. **Finish tape.** Flag 2's column acts like finish-line tape: as Maya's front touches it, the light snaps in the middle and two streamers of light flutter down onto the glass, then rise, rejoin and stand as the column again. *Twist:* the checkpoint was a finish line all along. A vertical ribbon of light gets broken like tape, and it resets itself for the next game.

20. **So close: the tape measure.** A dotted line of light unrolls from Theo and from Jun along the route each still needed (Theo's runs west to flag 1 and back up to flag 2), then each line snaps back into its robot like a tape measure, with a little recoil wobble. *Twist:* "how far were you?", told as a physical gag. The recoil gives each loser a tiny jolt.

21. **Skipping stone.** Maya is a Pebble, so she skips: she shoots off the board's edge across the dark room, rings of light blooming wherever she touches the black, and the skips curve round and bring her back onto the glass to stop exactly on flag 2, facing east. *Twist:* her form's pun, with the dark room acting like still water. Every form gets its own exit and return (an Orb rolls a lap of the rim, a Capsule pops open like a capsule toy, a Spire spins like a top), so the finale changes with the winner.

22. **Cairn.** Glass copies of Maya rise out of the glass one under another, lifting her up on a stack of her past selves like the cairn a walker builds at a summit (one stone per flag), then the stack sinks back into the glass and sets her down. *Twist:* the banned podium, rebuilt from her own history as her form's pun. The lab can build it from cloned models.

23. **Handshake line.** Theo and Jun drive over, each gives her a little clink of glass on glass with a spark in both colours, and they reverse back onto their squares along their own trails. *Twist:* sportsmanship, and the clink sounds like glasses raised in a toast. The trails show they went and came back, so the board stays truthful.

24. **The wink.** Once the ceremony quiets, Maya turns to face the camera, holds, and her eye-light dims and returns in one slow wink at the room before she turns back east. *Twist:* the winner acknowledges the couch, breaking the fourth wall with one tiny gesture.

25. **Victory dance, legal cards only.** Maya dances, and every step is a real program card popping over her head as she plays it: Rotate Right, Rotate Left, U-Turn, U-Turn, Back Up, Move 1, ending on the flag facing east. *Twist:* the whole dance is choreographed from Robo Rally's own deck and adds up to nothing, so the board state never changes.

26. **Winning hand.** Every card Maya played this round fans out above her as lit slides, like a poker hand laid on the table, with the final Move 2 glowing brightest in the middle, then they fold into one slide and slip into the glass. *Twist:* "winning hand", in a game where the program is the real hero.

27. **Ring toss.** The coloured ring at flag 1's foot (her proof of taking it) lifts off and sails across the board like a quoit, dropping over Maya with a clink, and flag 2's ring follows; after a beat both float back to their column feet. *Twist:* the checkpoint markers become a fairground ring-toss, and each clink counts one of her flags.

28. **Trick candles.** Maya "blows": a ripple crosses the glass from her front, and flag 1 and flag 2 flicker out like birthday candles with a curl of light-smoke, then after a beat of dark they sputter back to life, once, twice, like trick candles. *Twist:* it's a party game, the board is the cake, and the trick-candle relight both gets the laugh and puts the board back.

29. **The lid.** A huge sheet of dark glass slides over the slab like the box lid going on, swallowing Theo and Jun, and stops just short, leaving one slit of light on Maya for a breath before it slides away again. *Twist:* game over as packing up the box, with a spotlight made by the lid instead of a lamp.

30. **Domino run.** Thin glass panes stand up out of the grid lines and topple in a curving run from Maya's square, each one a chime a step higher, until the last gently taps Theo; then they sink back into the glass. *Twist:* the most satisfying thing in tabletop culture, rebuilt from glass and notes. The tap on Theo is the punchline.

31. **Her program is the encore.** The five cards Maya programmed this round play back as five glass-chime notes, each sending one soft wave across the board, and the column blooms on the last note (the Move 2). *Twist:* the winning program becomes the game's closing tune, and it's a different tune every game because every program is different.

32. **Heartbeat, then silence.** As Maya slides, every light on the board (columns, rings, eyes) pulses together in a slow heartbeat that quickens; when she touches the flag it all stops dead and dark for a full breath, then one clear chime and everything relights in her colour. *Twist:* the climax is silence, not a bang, so the room holds its breath with the board.

33. **Backing vocals.** Each robot's eye is a note: Jun's brightens and hums first, then Theo's a third higher, then Maya lands and completes the chord, and the three colours swell together and fade. *Twist:* the losers sing backup, and the chord changes with who is playing.

34. **The last centimetre.** Time slows to almost nothing as Maya reaches the column, her front a hair from the light, and the camera drifts round that held instant while a single mote floats past; then time snaps back and she touches. *Twist:* the slowed-down instant is the one before the win, not the win itself, so the room yells "go on!" at the TV.

35. **Ride-along.** The camera drops to robot height behind Maya and rides her last two spaces with her, the column swelling ahead like a finish line, then flies through the light and out into the dark room before floating back home. *Twist:* the whole room rides the winning move in first person.

36. **Through Theo's glass.** The camera cuts low behind Theo and looks across the board at Maya's column through the edge of his prism, so the win appears doubled, shifted and faintly blue. *Twist:* the win as the loser sees it, through the loser's own body.

37. **Carousel.** Maya stays perfectly still while the whole board turns beneath her like a turntable, carrying Theo and Jun round in a slow circle with their trails drawing arcs, until it stops one full turn later with everyone home. *Twist:* the banned victory spin turned inside out: she doesn't move, the world revolves around her.

38. **Rewind: the camera missed it.** The camera is looking at Jun when Maya wins and whips over too late, so the game rewinds: Maya slides backward, her trail sucked back into her, the column un-flashes, and the move plays again, properly framed. *Twist:* the instant replay done as tape rewind, with Lumen's trails retracting, and the camera is embarrassed. It still ends exactly on the win.

39. **Anamorphic.** Scattered slivers of light rise and hang over the board, meaningless, until the camera glides to one exact viewpoint and they click into a single huge "1" framing the column; the camera drifts on and they fall apart again. *Twist:* anamorphic street art (Varini-style) that only makes sense from one spot, revealed by the camera move.

40. **Photo finish.** A thin vertical line of light sweeps the board like a photo-finish camera and leaves a strip in the HUD where time is smeared sideways: Maya stretched and squeezed as she crossed, and far back in the strip, tiny Theo, two spaces away. *Twist:* a photo finish for a race that wasn't close, and the slit-scan distortion is funny and true (build it by copying one column of pixels from the canvas each frame).

41. **Self-timer.** A small light at the board's edge starts blinking faster and faster; Theo and Jun turn toward the camera (Jun only half makes it), a sheen sweeps the glass as the "shot" is taken, a print of that frame slides into the HUD, and they turn back. *Twist:* the family group photo, with a blurry loser in it.

42. **Droste.** The camera pulls back and back until the slab is a glowing rectangle in a dark living room, a TV, with the same board and Maya's column on it, then dives back in through the screen to home. *Twist:* the room watching a TV watches itself watching a TV (one render-target plane does it).

43. **Eyes in the dark.** As the room dims for the win, dozens of pairs of tiny warm eye-lights blink open in the darkness around the slab, an audience of robots who were always there; they blink in ripples like applause, then close. *Twist:* the dark room was never empty, and the applause is made of blinking.

44. **Lantern sky.** A copy of Maya's eye-light lifts off her and floats up into the dark like a sky lantern, and when the camera tilts to follow, other lanterns are already up there in other colours: tonight's earlier winners. *Twist:* the game remembers the whole game night, one lantern per game, so the third finale of the evening becomes a small constellation.

45. **House lights.** With a clunk the house lights come on: the dark room turns into a bare, too-bright grey studio, the glass looks like a cheap table and the robots' eyes shrink as they squint; a second later a click brings back the dark and the glow, column lit. *Twist:* the end-of-party lights, a deliberate break from the look that makes the return to Lumen feel luxurious.

46. **The race that would have been.** Faint ghost copies peel off Theo and Jun and race on in fast-forward, scrabbling to the flags to settle silver and bronze; they finish, glance at each other and fade, while the real robots never move. *Twist:* the game answers "who would have come second?" and then admits it doesn't matter.

47. **Autograph.** Maya zips off and draws a giant cursive M across the board with her trail in one flourish, a loop and an underline, then parks precisely back on flag 2 facing east while the signature glows and fades. *Twist:* the trail mechanic as calligraphy: the winner signs the board like a painting.

48. **Fireworks in reverse.** Sparks in every player's colour rush in from the dark toward a point above Maya and implode into one bright drop of light, which falls into her ring with a tiny plink. *Twist:* the cliché played backward, going from loud to quiet, with all the room's colours gathered into her.

49. **Afterimage.** For one frame the whole picture turns to a photographic negative (the room white, the glass black, Maya a cyan silhouette), then fades back over a second like the afterimage of looking at a bulb. *Twist:* it isn't a flash but a flash's afterimage, and it burns the moment into the eye (a CSS invert filter on the canvas, eased off).

50. **The last mote.** After the ceremony settles, one stray red mote drifts across the board, lands on Jun's top, and Jun's eye blinks; it's the last thing moving as the game carries on. *Twist:* a tiny coda for repeat viewers, and the only acknowledgement Jun gets all game.

## The five to animate

1. **Long exposure** (idea 1): the room points and laughs at the whole game's paths in one picture of light, which covers the board, comes from Lumen's own trails and is different every game.
2. **Skipping stone** (idea 21): a gasp ("she's off the edge!") that turns into a cheer when the winner breaks the deadliest rule and skips home across the dark, with a different lap for each form.
3. **House lights** (idea 45): the biggest laugh on the list, a full break from the look that changes the whole picture for a second and makes the return to Lumen feel luxurious.
4. **Eyes in the dark** (idea 43): wonder and warmth instead of a laugh, as a crowd of eye-lights applauds by blinking, readable at any size, and a reveal the room will look forward to every game.
5. **Domino run** (idea 30): the satisfying "again!" of a chain reaction made from the board's own wall fins, ending in a tiny tap on the rival, on a path that changes with where the rival is.
