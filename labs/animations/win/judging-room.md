# Win: the room's verdict

Moment: Maya (red) already has flag 1, plays Move 2, lands on flag 2 and wins the game while Theo and Jun are still racing. It happens once per game: the finale everyone is watching, with a 6 s budget. I judged it as six friends on a couch watching the TV, using the contact sheets and frames pulled from each video. Today's game flashes the flag 2 column red and sends two rings across the board while the camera eases in on her.

Because it plays only once a game, a bigger show is affordable here than for everyday moments. What matters most is the gasp or the laugh, and that the room never loses track of who won and where she stands.

## Winner: 1a-tape-measure

It's the only finale that is about this game: in half a second the whole race whips back onto the glass, the couch points at Theo's loop-the-loops and the spot where Jun went off the edge, then every trail reels into its robot with a clack, Maya's last. It's made only of Lumen's own trails, it's short (about 4.5 s), nobody moves, and because every game paints a different picture it won't wear out.

**Port:** Keep a per-robot log of every step: a fall as a gap with a drop off the edge, a restart at the reboot token, spins as small coils, with repeated passes offset so loops read as coils and not one thick line. Real histories are much longer than this curated one, so cap or simplify the oldest segments so a 12x12 board with up to 8 robots doesn't turn into spaghetti, and keep the speed per robot so all heads land together. Make Maya's route read as the winner's (brightest, last to reel in, the flag 2 column staying in her colour), hold the picture a little longer than 1.5 s since it plays once a game, and check the rocking robots settle exactly in place and facing.

## Runner-up: 2c-skipping-stone

The winner breaks the deadliest rule on the board: the room has watched robots die off edges all game, so 'she's off the edge!' is a real gasp, and the freeze and the rings of light on black water flip it into a cheer. It's the most beautiful, most Lumen picture in the set, and it still ends with her on flag 2 under a red pulse that crowns her.

**Port:** Swap the hard cut into the extreme close-up (it crops Theo and loses the board for about 2 s) for a fast push that keeps the slab edge and the winner in frame. Fade the zigzag trail off the edge as soon as she's back on the glass so nobody thinks she left the board. Work out the loop from the winner's real edge, facing and neighbours so it stays in frame, avoids other robots and pits, and works at corners, and build the per-form laps (Orb, Spire, Capsule and so on), because the skipping-stone pun only fits the Pebble.

## Also close

5b-domino-run is the safest crowd-pleaser (clean, legible, satisfying, and the path changes every game), but a domino run is the more familiar idea. 3c-house-lights gets the single biggest laugh with its folding table, though it leaves Lumen for most of its length and says nothing about the race that was played.
