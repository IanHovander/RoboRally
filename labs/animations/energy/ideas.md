# Energy (charging up on a battery): 50 animation ideas for Lumen

## Banned (too obvious)

- A bolt icon or "+1" popping up over the robot (what the game does today)
- Motes or particles of light rising off the robot (also today)
- Electric sparks, arcs or crackling around the robot
- The robot glowing brighter, or a burst of glow
- A battery meter or progress bar filling up over the robot
- A shockwave ring spreading out from the battery
- A beam or column of light shooting straight up from the battery
- The robot spinning or hopping for joy
- Camera shake or a screen flash
- A cube or coin flying into the HUD counter
- The robot buzzing or vibrating while it "charges"
- A plug and a cable

## Ideas

1. **The well.** As Maya settles, the battery's amber drains up into her and the battery goes dark, a ring of missing light like the way pits are drawn; a beat later one drop of warm light falls out of the black above the board, lands in the empty ring with a ripple, and the battery is lit again. *Twist:* it uses the board's own language (no light means empty) and answers a question nobody asked: why batteries never run out (someone up there keeps refilling them).
2. **Reeled in.** The red trail Maya just drew across two spaces doesn't fade. It snaps back into her tail like a tape measure retracting, and she rocks forward from the recoil as her eye warms to amber and back. *Twist:* the trail, which is normally just decoration, turns out to be the payment: she earned the energy by driving, and the board pays her in her own light.
3. **Regenerative braking.** Maya brakes hard on the battery while a glass ghost of her, drawn in amber light, carries on with her momentum half a space east; it slows, stops and springs back into her like a rubber band, and that snap is the charge. *Twist:* the energy is literally her stopping, the way an electric car recovers energy when it brakes, and the ghost shows the momentum she banked.
4. **From the floor.** On the dark room's floor far below, the faint spill of the board's light gathers into one amber point under the battery, and a hair-thin thread of light climbs up through the darkness and the smoked glass into Maya, then lets go and falls back. *Twist:* it reminds everyone that the slab is floating, and suggests the power comes from somewhere underneath the world.
5. **The reflection pays.** Maya's dim reflection in the glass lights up amber first and rises to meet her, and the two touch at the surface like palms on either side of a window; the light crosses over with a ripple and the reflection goes back to being a reflection. *Twist:* the mirror world under the glass is the power supply, and every polished robot turns out to have a twin down there.
6. **Eclipse.** Maya's body goes dark over the battery like the moon passing in front of the sun, and amber rays stream out across the glass from around her silhouette in a corona; then the rays fold in under her and only her eye is left a little warmer. *Twist:* the charge is drawn with darkness, which Lumen has as much of as light: the picture is the robot blocking the light.
7. **Burning glass.** The battery's light rises up through Maya as if she were a lens and comes out of her front as a tight, bright caustic on the glass at her pointer, scorching a tiny bolt there like a magnifying glass in the sun; the mark cools from white to amber to nothing. *Twist:* refraction draws the bolt instead of a pop-up, and it lands exactly where she is facing.
8. **Breath on the glass.** A frosted halo blooms across the glass around Maya like breath on a cold window, diffusing the battery's amber into a soft warm patch two spaces wide, then evaporates from the edges inward until the glass is clear again. *Twist:* warmth is shown the way glass actually shows it, by fogging up, which nobody expects from a game board.
9. **Slosh.** When Maya stops, the light under the glass sloshes like liquid in a tray: an amber swell runs out to the slab's edges, rebounds and converges back under her, lifting her a hair. On the way it passes under Ezra, who bobs like a cork and settles back facing north. *Twist:* the solid slab behaves like a full tray of liquid light, and the rival bobbing like a cork gets the laugh.
10. **Roots.** Fine amber veins creep out from the battery under the glass like frost or roots, reach the edge of Maya's ring and climb her shell like ivy, then withdraw into the glass the way they came. *Twist:* in a world of hard, clean geometry, energy grows like something alive, slow and organic instead of zappy.
11. **Chladni.** The glass hums like a struck plate: a fine dust of amber light on the battery's space jitters and settles into a Chladni figure (the pattern a vibrating plate draws in sand), whose last shape is the outline of a Pebble, and then the dust sweeps inward into Maya. *Twist:* real acoustics make the glass-chime sound visible, and the figure takes the shape of the robot being paid.
12. **She signs it.** Maya wiggles quickly in place and her eye, drawn as a long exposure, paints a zigzag bolt in the air above the battery like a sparkler in a night photo; the line hangs for half a second, then fades from its first stroke to its last. *Twist:* the bolt everyone expects is there, but she draws it herself in Lumen's long-exposure light instead of the game stamping an icon on her.
13. **She is the lamp.** The room's warm key light goes out for a second and Maya is the only light: a point light at her eye throws Ezra's long shadow across the glass, then the key light fades back up. *Twist:* instead of glowing brighter, she relights the whole scene, and every shadow on the board swings around her, so the room feels her power instead of being told about it.
14. **Big shadow, small robot.** The battery's light throws a huge silhouette of Maya onto the darkness behind the board, like shadow-play on fog, and the giant shadow rears up and puffs itself out while the real Maya just sits there; the shadow notices, sheepishly shrinks back and fades. *Twist:* her ego is projected, literally, using nothing but the dark room and one light.
15. **Wineglass rim.** A point of warm light starts circling Maya's ring on the glass, slowly at first and then faster, like a wet finger round the rim of a wineglass; as it speeds up the ring warms toward amber, sings for an instant at full speed and settles back to red. *Twist:* it's a glass harmonica, the right instrument for Lumen's glass sounds, and the charge is the pitch rising.
16. **Skipping stone.** Maya is a Pebble, so her last space is a skimmed stone: two low, flat hops, each touch leaving a ripple ring in the glass, and the third touch lands dead centre on the battery with a ripple that comes out amber. *Twist:* it's a pun on her form and on glass as water, and it happens inside the move itself, so it costs no extra time.
17. **Toaster.** Maya sinks a quarter of her height into the battery while its three arcs glow hotter around her like toaster elements; a beat, then *ding*: she pops up in a little hop and lands on the space a shade warmer as the arcs cool. *Twist:* the most ordinary kitchen appliance there is, played straight; the beat of waiting before the pop is the joke.
18. **Warm spot.** Maya settles on the battery like a cat on a patch of sun. She flattens a little, her eye narrows to a contented slit and the edge of her ring ripples as if purring, then she sits up ready to go. *Twist:* the battery is a warm hearthstone and the robot has a cat's priorities; the purr is drawn in her own ring of light.
19. **Hiccup.** The charge goes in smoothly, then Maya hiccups: a tiny involuntary hop, and one small bubble of amber light pops out of her eye, floats up a robot-height and bursts. *Twist:* the cliché cloud of particles shrinks to a single burped bubble, which hints that the polished pebble has insides.
20. **Static.** As the charge lands, hair-thin filaments of light stand up all over Maya like hair rubbed with a balloon while her eye squints, then they flop down one by one and fade; across the board a single strand stands up on the tip of Ezra's spire and droops. *Twist:* electricity is drawn as static frizz instead of sparks, with a second, tinier laugh on the neighbour for anyone watching closely.
21. **Gulp.** A bead of amber light floats up out of the battery to Maya's eye as if to a mouth; she leans in and swallows it, and a bulge of light travels back along her body like an egg going down a snake, fading out at her tail. *Twist:* a cartoon swallow on a robot with no mouth, and you can follow the energy's whole trip into her from across the room.
22. **Shake-off.** The charge soaks into Maya and she shakes off the excess like a wet dog: a fast shiver from nose to tail that flings a few amber droplets onto the glass around her, where they sit as little dots of light and fade. *Twist:* it reads as invigorated, and the droplets on the glass are a small echo that rewards a second look.
23. **Balloon.** Maya fills, swells round and floats up off the glass like an overfilled balloon, her ring stretching up under her like a string; a pinprick hiss of warm air from her back lets her drift back down onto the battery at her normal size. *Twist:* "max 10" taken literally, as if robots had a fill line, with the hiss as a perfectly timed deflation.
24. **Vending machine.** Maya stops on the battery and nothing happens. She looks down at it, lifts and thumps down on it once, and the battery coughs up the charge with a clunk, its arcs flaring. *Twist:* it's accurate to the rules (energy comes at the end of the register, not on arrival), and everyone in the room has thumped a vending machine.
25. **Parking sensor.** As Maya rolls onto the battery, a small light on her tail blinks faster and faster as she nears the centre of the space, like a car's parking sensor, and turns solid at dead centre; the battery answers with a warm glow up into her. *Twist:* landing exactly on the space becomes the drama, told with a rhythm everyone knows from real cars.
26. **Running on fumes.** If Maya arrives low on energy, she arrives tired: her eye dim and flickering like a phone at 1%, the last half space sluggish, her body sagging. The battery catches her, her eye snaps open and she straightens. *Twist:* the arrival tells the room how broke she was (the lower her energy, the sleepier she drives), while a rich robot just rolls in perky and sips.
27. **Wink.** After the charge, Maya leans toward the camera without turning (her pointer still says east) and winks at the room, her eye squashing shut and opening again with a tiny glint. *Twist:* the fourth wall breaks for a quarter of a second, aimed at whoever is holding Maya's phone, and their friends will notice.
28. **The neighbour.** Across the board, Ezra's Spire leans toward Maya the way you'd peer at someone else's lottery ticket, its eye brightening a hair; when the charge lands it straightens up and pointedly faces north again. *Twist:* the other robots are an audience with feelings: the moment is Maya's but the laugh is on Ezra, and it changes with whoever happens to be nearby.
29. **Six forms, six ways to drink.** Each form takes energy its own way: the Pebble swells round like a sponge, the Orb spins it in like thread onto a spool, the Prism splits the amber into a spectrum and folds it into its own colour, the Capsule fills like a vial, the Cube lights up face by face, and the Spire conducts it up to its tip. Maya, a Pebble, swells and relaxes in about a second. *Twist:* a frequent moment looks different for every player, and each version says something about what that robot is.
30. **The bolt hops.** The bolt drawn under the glass peels itself off the battery, stands up like a little zigzag creature, hops onto Maya and dissolves into her; a moment later a fresh bolt fades back into the ring under her. *Twist:* the board's icon is alive and has a job, and when it walks back in, the room knows the battery is still there for the next robot.
31. **The hug.** The battery's three amber arcs lift off the glass, rise around Maya and close into a ring that squeezes her once, so she bulges comically at the top; then they let go and slide back down flat into the glass. *Twist:* the board element is affectionate, and the hug is made from its own drawing (three arcs) lifted into 3D.
32. **The banker's hand.** An amber glass cube slants in from the camera's side, as if an unseen hand in the room had placed it, sets down beside Maya with a glass clack and slides into her. Every so often the hand fumbles: the cube bounces, rolls into the battery and shoots up into her from below. *Twist:* in the cardboard game, energy is a cube someone hands you; here it seems to come from one of the people watching.
33. **Curling.** Just off the board's edge, a little stack of amber cubes (the bank) glimmers in the dark; the top one slides off and skates across the glass like a curling stone with a thin streak behind it, stops perfectly inside Maya's ring and sinks in, and the bank fades away. *Twist:* it pairs the board game's supply with a sport everyone half understands; the long glide and the perfect stop are what satisfy.
34. **The tower.** A small tower of glass cubes appears above Maya, one for each energy she already has, and the new cube drops onto the top with a Jenga wobble; once it steadies, the tower telescopes down into her. *Twist:* it shows the total, not just "+1", and the wobble is a moment of suspense about something that cannot fail.
35. **The tiniest column.** A checkpoint-style column of light starts to rise from the battery but stops a little taller than Maya, stubby; it pops like a cork, and the cap flies up into the dark and comes back down as one glass cube that clinks onto her and melts in. *Twist:* a self-aware parody of the big flag moment: the board knows a battery is a small reward and jokes about it, which keeps the real columns special.
36. **Stamped.** In the HUD, the Move 2 card that brought Maya here gets a small amber bolt stamped on its corner with a little thud, while on the board her eye warms once. *Twist:* it credits the play rather than the robot, so the player sees their card choice rewarded, and choosing cards is the real skill in Robo Rally.
37. **Over the edge.** A drop of amber light leaves the battery, runs under the glass to the near edge of the slab, hangs there like a drip and falls off into the dark; a beat later Maya's energy number in the HUD ticks up with a small splash. *Twist:* the banned "fly to the counter" becomes gravity, as if the HUD sits just below the board, and the half second of falling is the comedy.
38. **Tree rings.** When Maya rolls on, the battery briefly shows each robot that has charged on it this game as a thin coloured ring, oldest in the middle like the rings of a tree; Maya's red ring is added on the outside and then they all fade. *Twist:* the board has a memory: the rings build up as the game goes on, which rewards anyone watching all night and settles arguments about who has been camping on it.
39. **The pump.** Thin numbers in the board's own type appear on the glass beside the battery and spin like a petrol-pump dial, a whirl of decimals slowing and slowing until, clunk, they stop on exactly 1.00 and fade. *Twist:* an enormous fuss over one unit, with the pump's satisfying clunk as the punchline.
40. **Arpeggio.** The battery's three arcs light one after another under Maya like three rising glass notes, her eye blinking on each, and her ring answers with a chord on the fourth beat. *Twist:* the battery's drawing was always a little instrument, and if the starting note follows her energy, each robot's batteries climb a scale through the game until the tenth cube completes the octave.
41. **Drum roll.** The battery's arcs and Maya's ring flicker in a fast, accelerating drum roll that builds and builds, and the payoff is the smallest possible blink of amber in her eye, right on the rimshot. *Twist:* a comedy rimshot for a small reward: a big build and a tiny punchline, short enough to stay funny.
42. **Count-blinks.** Maya's eye blinks out her new total in soft amber, a long blink for five and a short blink for each one after that, like a phone showing its battery level when you plug it in; at 10 it holds one long, steady glow instead. *Twist:* it's real information with no number on screen, and regulars learn to count each other's blinks.
43. **Only the light moves.** Time stops for everything except the energy: Ezra's shimmer, the fading trail and Maya herself freeze mid-settle while a slow amber current flows unhurriedly from the battery up into her; once it's in, the world unfreezes. *Twist:* the freeze isn't for drama but to show what energy is here, the only moving thing in a still world, calm rather than explosive.
44. **Polite cutaway.** As Maya settles, the camera turns politely away, drifting a little toward Ezra as if to give her privacy; it drifts back and she is sitting there a shade warmer, eye half closed, clearly pleased. *Twist:* it's the film cutaway, in a party game: not showing it is funnier than showing it (the game's sound can play a soft glug), and the drift is small enough to keep the board readable.
45. **Overnight.** The board lives through a night in one second: the key light swings across like the sun in a time-lapse, shadows sweep round Maya and Ezra, and Maya sits with her eye dimmed like a phone on a nightstand; at "morning" her eye opens bright. *Twist:* she charged overnight, the way everyone charges their phone, squeezed into a blink.
46. **Brownout.** The instant the charge goes in, the whole picture (board, robots and HUD) dims and flickers once, like house lights when a big appliance switches on, and Ezra's eye flickers too, as if on the same circuit; then everything recovers. *Twist:* the opposite of a flash: Maya pulled so much power that the TV itself seemed to sag.
47. **One too many.** Two amber cubes rise out of the battery and both dive into Maya; a beat later one pops back out of her, like a coin a machine spits back, and sinks into the battery. *Twist:* the game enforces "+1" on screen, so the rule becomes something you watch being checked, and she clearly tried.
48. **Fake-out.** The amber charge leaves the battery under the glass and slides the wrong way, toward Ezra, who perks up; halfway there it stops, turns back as if remembering whose space this is, and dives into Maya while Ezra deflates. *Twist:* half a second of suspense over a rule everyone knows, and the right robot always gets it.
49. **The glint.** For a few frames the highlight on Maya's polished shell takes the shape of a small bolt, the battery's bolt reflected in her finish like the glint on a cartoon smile, while her HUD number ticks up, and that is the whole animation. *Twist:* it's the quietest version, made only of reflection: people notice it the fifth time and then look for it every time.
50. **The ladder.** The animation grows with how much energy she has: at 1-3 a quick sip, at 4-6 a gulp and a happy bounce, at 7-9 the glass around her hums and her ring brightens, and at the tenth cube the camera dips to look up through the slab as light pours into her from below and the overflow runs across the glass and spills off the edge. *Twist:* the room can read how rich anyone is from how the battery treats them, and "full" gets a spectacular beat because it really is rare.
