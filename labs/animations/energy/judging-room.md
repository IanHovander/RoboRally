## Winner: 2c-brownout

The whole TV sags and only Maya stays lit, her ring blazing amber while her energy number ticks, so everyone on the couch sees who charged, even the ones looking at their phones. It moves no robot and the dip is over in about half a second, so it can come back all night as a running joke ('Maya's draining the grid again'). It is made of nothing but Lumen's light and darkness.

**Port:** Start the dip only once she has stopped: in the render it hits at about 1.23 s while she is still braking. Borrow 2a's Ezra gag (the other robots wilt and come back a beat late) to add the laugh 2c doesn't have. Cap the depth when several robots charge at once so the board and the other robots never fully disappear. Keep the flicker to one down, half-back, down, with no fast strobing (photosensitivity). Drive the real HUD tokens and scene lights, and restore every intensity exactly afterwards.

## Runner-up: 3a-bankers-hand

An amber die lobbed from our side of the screen comes from a direction nothing else in the game does. It is big enough to read from across the room, and it is literally the cardboard game's energy cube. The one-in-four fumble keeps it from turning into wallpaper.

**Port:** Shorten the bounce after it lands, and make the skid end with the cube visibly bumping into her and melting into her (right now it melts beside her before she glows). Keep the cube from crossing her silhouette while it is at its largest. At 2.4 s it is at the edge of the 2.5 s budget, so trim it. When several robots charge on one register, stagger the tosses.
