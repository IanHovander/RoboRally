# Gear: 50 ideas for a quarter turn (Lumen)

Maya (red Pebble) stands on the green gear at (3,1) and turns 90° right, north to east. Theo (blue Prism) watches
from (1,3). The budget is 2 s and the moment comes up often. Every idea ends with Maya on her space facing east,
Theo untouched and the camera home.

## Banned (too obvious)

- Sparks or glints flying off the gear's teeth.
- A POW / WHIRR / CLICK word, or a "90°" label popping up.
- A plain smooth spin with motion blur or speed lines.
- A camera shake or jolt on the click.
- A glow burst or ring pulse when the gear fires (today's brighten-and-fade, only louder).
- An overshoot-and-settle springy wobble at the end of the turn.
- Dizzy stars, spiral eyes or a woozy wobble afterwards.
- A pirouette: several full turns before stopping on east.
- Literal metal cogs meshing, ratchet clicks, clockwork teeth.
- A big curved arrow overlay showing the direction.
- The camera orbiting the robot while it turns.
- A DJ turntable / record scratch, or the robot being screwed into the floor.

## Ideas

1. **Her pointer gets there first.** The gear grabs what lies on the glass, her ring of light and its pointer, and swings them east while her body keeps facing north, so for a beat her pointer says east and her eye says north. Then she notices and hurries round after it with a little hop, landing in line (1.3 s). *Twist:* her parts disagree: the light on the glass belongs to the board, and the body belongs to her.

2. **Tablecloth trick.** The gear yanks a full, fast turn under her, a blur of mint ticks with a tablecloth "fwip", and Maya doesn't move a millimetre. After a held beat of perfect stillness she turns the quarter herself, slowly and with dignity (1.6 s). *Twist:* the magic trick works, and she still obeys the rule, on her own terms.

3. **Indicating right.** A small amber light blinks twice on her right flank, tick-tock, in exactly the rhythm of the gear's clicks, and then she pulls round smoothly to east. On a red gear the blinker is on her left (1.2 s). *Twist:* road manners. The room knows the direction half a second before it happens, which is also the best rules lesson a gear can give.

4. **Spotting.** Her eye light stays locked on north while her body turns underneath it, the light sliding round her polished shell. Then it whips round to her new front with a bright glint, the way a dancer spots a pirouette (1.0 s). *Twist:* real ballet technique on a pebble, and the eye suddenly reads as a head.

5. **Quarter past.** The gear is drawn with twelve ticks, so it becomes a clock face: as her pointer sweeps, the ticks at 1, 2 and 3 o'clock light up under it like a minute hand passing. At 3 a soft glass chime strikes the register number, three chimes in register 3 (1.6 s). *Twist:* gears were always clocks. A quarter turn is literally a quarter past, and the chimes tell you where you are in the round.

6. **Second hand.** Instead of gliding, she clicks round in three crisp 30° steps, one per gear tick. Each step is a glass note, a rising major triad on green gears and a falling minor one on red, and her ring blinks on every step (0.9 s). *Twist:* you can hear a gear with your eyes closed: right sounds bright and rising, left sounds the opposite.

7. **The gear's song.** Her pointer is the pin of a music box and the gear's ticks are its comb: each tick she passes lights up and plucks a note. Every gear turn in the game plays the next three notes of the same melody (1.0 s). *Twist:* a moment that happens dozens of times becomes a serial. By the last round the room has heard the whole tune come together, and someone is humming it.

8. **Singing glass.** Her ring rubs the gear like a wet finger round a wine glass. A pure tone swells, and the smoked glass under her shimmers into a standing-wave pattern of light that settles into a four-petal cross pointing north, east, south and west. When she stops on east, the tone and the pattern fade (1.8 s). *Twist:* Lumen's one material actually sings, and its vibration draws a compass.

9. **Lighthouse.** The room dims a touch and her eye throws a long beam of warm light through the haze of the dark room. It sweeps from north across the right of the board, glinting off the board's edge, and comes to rest on the spaces she now faces before folding back into her eye (1.5 s). *Twist:* the beam makes the dark room's air visible for a beat, and it previews her next move: if it lands on a pit, the groans start early.

10. **The lens knows.** A disc of glass rises out of the slab over her like a magnifier. Through it, refracted, Maya already faces east while the real Maya still faces north; then the lens sinks back and the real Maya catches up with her image (1.4 s). *Twist:* refraction shows the future, and reality has to follow the glass.

11. **Leaf on a pond.** The gear's light loosens into a slow circular current under the glass, the light of the belts' streams curled into a whirlpool. Maya, weightless, drifts round on it with a gentle bob, completely passive, and comes to rest facing east as the current calms (1.8 s). *Twist:* the calmest possible version: nobody turns her, she simply floats round.

12. **Crossed filters.** As she turns, the gear's patch of glass darkens like two polarising filters being crossed. At exactly 90° it goes black, a hole where the light is missing, which is exactly what a pit looks like. The room gasps, then the light floods back and she's fine (1.4 s). *Twist:* real optics doubles as a jump scare: the pit that wasn't.

13. **Nut on a bolt.** A slim column of light, finer than a checkpoint's, rises through her with a faint spiral groove. She lifts a little way up it and runs back down the spiral like a nut down a bolt, which has turned her exactly a quarter by the time she touches the glass, and the column withdraws (1.6 s). *Twist:* the turn stops being an instruction and becomes the plain physics of a screw thread.

14. **Light with weight.** As her eye sweeps, it leaves a quarter-arc of light hanging in the air at eye height, like a long-exposure light painting. When she stops, the arc drifts down like a ribbon, settles on the glass and becomes part of her trail, fading as trails do (1.8 s). *Twist:* light that falls. Lumen's trails turn out to be made of something that has to land somewhere.

15. **Fogged window.** Her glow pool breathes on the glass and mists it. Her pointer, like a fingertip, draws a curved arrow from north to east in the mist, and she turns to follow her own drawing as the mist evaporates (1.8 s). *Twist:* everyone has drawn on a misted car window. The robot writes its own instructions and then obeys them.

16. **Glass caseback.** Her polish turns clear for a beat, and the gear's mint ring rises up inside her, spinning like a watch movement behind a glass caseback and turning her from within. Then the ring sinks back to the glass and her colour returns (1.5 s). *Twist:* a peek inside the robot finds clockwork made of light instead of brass.

17. **Molten.** She melts into a bright puddle of red light on the glass, the puddle swirls a quarter round the gear, and she pours back up into her shape, now facing east (1.6 s). *Twist:* the old myth that glass is a slow liquid, sped up: she was never solid, only patient.

18. **Lights out.** The room drops to black for half a second and only the lights remain: her eye and pointer sweep a quarter circle like two fireflies, and Theo's eye blinks once in the dark. The lights come back up and she faces east (1.2 s). *Twist:* the turn is told purely in light, and Theo's blink in the dark rewards a second look.

19. **Red plus mint.** Mint light rises from the gear into her red body as she turns. Halfway round the two mix into warm white, Lumen's own light, so for an instant she is a white pebble; then the mint drains back into the gear and she's red again, facing east (1.2 s). *Twist:* real additive colour. Red plus its complement makes white, so the gear and the robot together make the house light.

20. **Afterimage.** When she lands on east, her old north-pointing pointer lingers on the glass as a faint cyan ghost, the colour your own eye would burn in after a red lamp, and it fades within a second (1.2 s). *Twist:* the TV fakes the afterimage your retina makes, subtly enough that people wonder whether they saw it.

21. **Pole star.** Points of warm light appear in the dark above the board and draw short concentric arcs around the gear's axis, like a long-exposure photo of the night sky wheeling round Polaris. They fade as she stops (1.8 s). *Twist:* for a beat the whole sky turns around a small red pebble, a grand gesture for a mundane moment, and the sky is gone before it can argue with the look.

22. **Tea leaves.** The faint motes of light in the gear swirl as she turns and, against intuition, gather toward her centre instead of flying outward, pooling into her glow (1.2 s). *Twist:* this is the tea-leaf paradox, the real physics of a stirred cup: tiny, true and weird.

23. **Spaghetti fork.** Her trail, still glowing on the glass from how she arrived, winds round her as she turns, a coil of red light hugging her ring like spaghetti on a fork. When she stops it unspools and fades as usual (1.6 s). *Twist:* Lumen's trail behaves like a physical string. It only happens when she arrived leaving a trail, so the moment varies by itself.

24. **Find the real Maya.** Glass copies slide out of her like reflections in a kaleidoscope, and the four Mayas shuffle round each other inside her square like cups in a shell game. Three dissolve into light, and the one left, facing east, is the real one (1.8 s). *Twist:* the room plays along without being asked ("that one!"), and the answer is always east.

25. **Dimmer switch.** As she turns right, the whole room comes up, as if a dimmer knob were turning: the glass brightens and the floor of the dark room far below catches the spill. A beat later it eases back down, as if someone in the room had turned it back. Red gears dim the room instead (1.8 s). *Twist:* the robot is the room's light switch, and people glance at their own lamps.

26. **Coin on a table.** She tips onto her rim and wobbles like a coin settling: the wobble circles faster and the whirr rises in pitch while her heading creeps a quarter round. Then she drops flat facing east and the sound cuts to silence (1.6 s). *Twist:* everyone knows the sound of a coin spinning down on a table, and the sudden silence is the punchline.

27. **Roly-poly.** She takes "turn right" literally and tips over onto her right side. Then, like a roly-poly toy, she rocks back upright, each rock swinging her heading a little further round, until she settles facing east (1.7 s). *Twist:* a misunderstanding, solved by toy physics.

28. **Circling the basket.** Like a dog settling into its bed, she turns a little past east, dips her nose and backs up, then flops down facing east with a soft squash. Her eye dims contentedly as the gear's light softens under her like a cushion (1.9 s). *Twist:* the gear is a round dog bed, and she makes herself at home on it.

29. **A lap of her own square.** She slides out toward the edge of her space and circles it once clockwise, like a coin in a wishing-well funnel. Her nose follows the curve as she spirals inward and speeds up, and she arrives at the centre facing east, leaving a little spiral on the glass that fades (1.8 s). *Twist:* she goes on a whole journey without ever leaving her space, which is exactly the rule, and the spiral says "a gear happened here".

30. **Laser-pointer cat.** A dot of mint light skitters out of the gear round the front of her ring. She tracks it in jerky stop-start turns, wiggles and pounces with a hop in place, and the dot vanishes under her nose, which now points east (1.8 s). *Twist:* the robot is a cat, and the gear knows exactly how to steer one.

31. **Copycat.** Watching her, Theo starts turning too, out of sympathy. The glass under him gives a soft flicker of "no", because he isn't on a gear, so he swings back to east, sheepish, his eye dimming, while she completes her turn and they end up facing the same way (1.6 s). *Twist:* the board enforces the rule (only gears turn you) as social comedy.

32. **Is anyone watching?** Just before turning, she tilts back and turns her eye toward the camera for a quarter of a second, looking straight at the room like an actor breaking the fourth wall. Then she turns east as if nothing happened (1.2 s). *Twist:* the robots know they're on TV.

33. **Six ways round.** Each form turns a quarter its own way: the Pebble glides like a curling stone, the Orb just slides its eye round its equator, the Prism flashes a facet glint every 30°, the Capsule swings like a moored boat, and the Spire sweeps its lit tip through the air like a conductor's baton. The Cube turns too, but because a quarter turn leaves a cube looking identical, only its eye hopping from one face to the next shows it (about 1 s each). *Twist:* the geometry makes the joke. For the Cube a 90° turn is invisible, so the eye alone says anything changed.

34. **Party spinner.** The gear's ticks become a party-game spinner and Maya is its arrow. She gets flicked, spins round once and a bit, slows tick by tick, nearly stops on north-east, creeps, and clicks onto east, where thin type reads "right · green" (1.9 s). *Twist:* all the suspense of chance with no chance in it. The rule always wins, and "green means right" sticks.

35. **Fig. 7.** The camera rises to look straight down and the board flattens into a rulebook diagram: a dotted arc, a printed arrow and a thin caption, "Fig. 7: a green gear turns a robot 90° to the right". She turns as the diagram says, and the camera sinks back home (2.0 s; for the game's first gear, or rarely). *Twist:* for a moment the game becomes its own rulebook, which board-gamers will love.

36. **The board plays a card.** A lit slide of the Right Turn card, the same card the players hold on their phones, rises out of the gear, flips to face the room and dissolves into Maya, who turns (1.6 s). *Twist:* the board is a player too, with a hand of its own, and everyone recognises the card.

37. **A hand in the dark.** A hand-shaped shadow, made of missing light like a pit, reaches over the glass from off-screen. It pinches Maya like a game piece, twists her a quarter and withdraws (1.6 s). *Twist:* it's a ghost of the cardboard board game, where you turned the figures by hand, drawn in Lumen's own darkness.

38. **Pinch to rotate.** Two soft fingertip dots of light, like the touch markers in a screen recording, press onto the glass on either side of her, twist a quarter and lift away, and she turns with them (1.2 s). *Twist:* the glass slab is a giant touchscreen, and everyone in the room has made that gesture today.

39. **Buffering.** Halfway round she freezes, and the gear's twelve ticks start chasing round in fading steps, exactly like a loading spinner. Half a second later it "loads" and she finishes the turn (1.6 s; use sparingly). *Twist:* the gear was drawn with twelve ticks all along. The game pretends to lag, and the room laughs at its own TV.

40. **Product shot.** The room dims and a long soft highlight slides across her polished shell as she turns, like a car on a showroom turntable, while thin type fades in under her: "Maya. Now facing east." (1.9 s). *Twist:* the most boring move in the game gets a luxury reveal.

41. **Who turned?** Maya refuses to turn, so the camera swings round her instead: she stays still on screen while the whole board wheels past. Then the camera eases home and, from where the room sits, she faces east (2.0 s; rare). *Twist:* relativity as stubbornness. From her point of view, the world turned.

42. **Wrong way, Maya.** She starts turning left, and the gear's arrowhead flares with a gentle double tick, "uh-uh". She stops, reverses and turns right to east, a touch quicker and a touch dimmer, embarrassed (1.5 s). *Twist:* the board corrects her and teaches the direction in the same breath, and the end is exactly the rule.

43. **Ship of Theseus.** A clear glass Maya, already facing east, rises out of the gear as the old one sinks into the glass, the two crossing like lifts at half height. The new one fills with red, and her name and trail carry on (1.6 s). *Twist:* the rule says the gear rotates her, but it replaces her instead. Is it still Maya? The game says yes.

44. **Moving north.** A thin "N" sits at the board's far edge. When the gear fires, the N slides along the edge and round to the right-hand side, and Maya, a compass needle, swings to follow it; then the N slides quietly back to the top while she stays pointing east (1.8 s). *Twist:* the gear moves north and fools the compass, and she ends up exactly where the rules want her.

45. **Domino ticks.** The three gear ticks between her nose and east stand up out of the glass as small bright fins, like Lumen's walls. They topple one after another, a wave of falling light that pushes her pointer round, and then lie flat again as part of the gear's drawing (1.3 s). *Twist:* the gear's markings were dominoes all along, and a chain reaction does the turning.

46. **A curl in the trail.** After the turn, the end of her trail on the glass bends into a small quarter-curl that ends in her new direction, and it fades with the rest of the trail (1.0 s, plus the usual fade). *Twist:* trails normally record only where she went. Now they also record how she turned, so someone glancing up later can still read "she turned here".

47. **The gear remembers.** Each time the gear turns someone, a faint quarter-arc in that robot's colour stays inside its ring, so over the game it fills like a pie chart of who's been spun. When four quarters complete a circle, the gear rings once like a struck bell and the arcs fade, ready to fill again (1.2 s). *Twist:* the board keeps a history, and regulars start watching to see which colour closes the circle.

48. **Breaking in.** The first gear turn of the night is stiff: jerky, with a squeaky glass note and the gear's light flickering with effort. Each later turn is a little smoother and richer, until by the last round it's one buttery glide with a full chord (0.8–1.4 s). *Twist:* the board warms up along with the room, and nobody notices until they compare round one with round ten.

49. **The inscription.** The first time any gear acts in a game, a line of thin type writes itself round the gear's rim as she turns, like the lettering on a coin: "green gears turn right". Then it fades and never appears again (1.6 s). *Twist:* the board teaches the rule once, at the moment it matters, and then trusts you.

50. **Stop-motion.** For the turn, the world drops to eight frames a second: Maya rotates in crisp steps, her ring and glow jittering a hair between frames like a clay figure an animator is handling. Then everything snaps back to silky smoothness (1.0 s). *Twist:* a deliberate break in the look, a handmade moment in a world of light, and it comes straight home.

## The five to animate

1. **Laser-pointer cat** (30): the surest laugh. Everyone has watched a cat chase a dot, it reads in silhouette, and the seeded feints keep the running gag fresh.
2. **A hand in the dark** (37): the gasp. A giant from outside the game reaches in, drawn in Lumen's own darkness, and a hand-sized shadow is readable from any sofa.
3. **Lighthouse** (9): the anticipation. The beam shows what she now faces, so the room groans or cheers early, and it is useful every single time.
4. **Coin on a table** (26): the satisfying one. A familiar object spins down faster and faster to a dead stop, a rhythm people want to see again, with no character and nothing added.
5. **Singing glass** (8): the beauty. The one material sings and draws a compass with the needle on east, a calm "ooh" that spreads wide enough to read on a TV.
