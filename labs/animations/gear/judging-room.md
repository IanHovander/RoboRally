# Gear: the room's verdict

I judged as six friends on a couch watching the TV between turns. The moment: Maya stands on a green gear and is
turned 90° right, from north to east. Gears are common on most courses, so this plays many times a night, and
several robots can turn in the same register. The budget is 2 s. Every entry fits it, with about 1.6 to 1.9 s of
action, against about 0.7 s for today's plain turn in place (baseline).

I judged from the contact sheets plus my own frame pulls from every video (10 to 30 fps, full frame and cropped
on Maya, plus side-by-side stills of the two strongest hands), always asking what reads at TV size from across the
room.

## Winner: 2c-hand-vertigo (The hand, in vertigo)

**Why.** This is the one the room will talk about. A player's hand reaching into the TV to turn the piece, as in
the cardboard game, is a real surprise, and with the lamp pool behind it the hand reads instantly from the couch.
Unlike the cat or the coin, the joke is the rule itself: you watch fingers close on Maya and turn her a quarter to
the right, so nobody misses what happened, and "here comes the hand" becomes a call-out the room joins in on.

**Port: what to fix or watch.**
- **Several gears at once.** Gears fire for every robot standing on one at the same time, so decide what happens
  when two or three are turned in one register. Either each robot gets its own hand from its nearest edge, angled
  away from the others, or one hand visits each robot in turn within the 2 s budget.
- **Lamp pool colour.** Pull it toward Lumen's warm white on smoked glass. Right now it turns the board into a beige
  tabletop.
- **Wear by the 20th gear.** Keep the vertigo lean subtle. After the first few gears of a game, consider a shorter
  version: about 1.2 s with no camera move.
- **Hand from our side.** When the nearest edge is our side of the board, check that the forearm doesn't cover
  other robots or their labels.
- **Red gears and visibility.** Mirror the twist on red gears. Keep Maya and her pointer visible inside the grip, as
  they are now.

## Runner-up: 5b-the-note (Finding the note)

**Why.** The quiet favourite: the glass sings, the light hunts and then locks into a compass pointing where she'll
end up, and she turns into it, so the turn is shown twice. It's made entirely of Lumen's glass and light, it's
clean, and it will still be lovely on the 20th gear of the night, when the louder ideas might wear thin. It's the
safe and beautiful fallback if the hand turns out to be too much.

**Port: what to fix or watch.**
- **Red gears.** The hunting ripples are mint, the same green as a right-turn gear. On red gears, use the left-turn
  colour and put the long petal west.
- **The long petal.** Make it clearly longer at TV size, since it's what carries the rule.
- **Its footprint.** The figure spans about three spaces. Keep it under the robots and faint enough not to hide
  neighbouring belts, pits or lasers, and work out how two figures blend when adjacent robots turn at once.
- **Theo.** Drop his sway and hop. They can't be seen at this size, and other robots shouldn't move during someone
  else's moment.
- **Sound.** Time the tone's swell to the moment the figure locks.
