Budget is 2 s, and the gear is common, so how well it repeats weighs heavily. The videos have no sound, so any idea whose punchline is a sound is judged on screen only.

## Winner: 5b-the-note

It is the one that belongs in Lumen: the glass rings, light gathers onto the still lines and locks into a compass that announces her new heading before she turns. It is calm and beautiful, and it says something useful, so it survives being seen ten times a night. The result reads clearly at TV size (a bright figure three spaces across), and the surprise (glass that sings) is fresh rather than borrowed.

**Porting:** Fix the end pop: fade the figure out over about 0.25 s and cross-fade Maya's ring glow back instead of restoring it in one frame. Give the fade-in at least 0.15 s. Make the east petal clearly dominant (brighter and longer, and shorten the west petal) so it reads as 'east', not 'east-west'. Trim the restless hunt to about 0.4 s so the moment lands near 1.5 s. Port the figure as one additive shader plane on the glass, under the robots' depth, and cap its radius at about 1.5 spaces so that robots on neighbouring gears blend rather than stack. Drop or minimise Theo's sway and hop, because a robot that moves when the rules don't move it misleads the room. Map PALETTE.right/left/light to scene.js's colours and check that the red-gear mirror points west.

## Runner-up: 1b-cat-and-dot

It is the biggest laugh with the cleanest staging: one bright dot is the focal point, she faces east by about 1.3 s, and the crouch-wiggle-pounce is a joke the whole room knows. Of the three cat versions, it has the clearest beats and the cleanest ending, without 1a's rubbery distortion or 1c's camera cuts.

**Porting:** Scale the acting up for a 10-13 wide board: the crouch and wiggle are small even here, and in the game Maya is about half this size, so push the poses and keep the dot big and bright. Keep her ring flat on the glass while she leans (it clips into the glass in the lab). Seed the feints so they vary, and consider a shorter cut (about 1.2 s, without the stalk) for repeats in the same game, so the gag doesn't wear out. With several robots on gears at once, launch each dot from its own gear arrow and keep the dots apart. Theo's blink needs an eye-blink hook on other robots in scene.js; leave it out if that isn't cheap.

## In short

5b and 1b tie on overall score; 5b wins on fit. The gear happens all night, and Lumen is calm light on glass: a compass of light that shows the heading holds up on the tenth spin, and a cat routine may not. The hand (2c) is the best surprise in the set, but its beige pool, brightness jump and camera move make it a one-time event, not a rule animation.
