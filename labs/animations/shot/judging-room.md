# Getting shot: the room's verdict

Judged as six friends on a couch watching the TV between turns. This moment happens every register for every robot in someone's line of fire, which makes it the most frequent bad thing in a game, and it has a 2.5 s budget. What the game shows today (baseline) is a plain beam that fades in about 1 s. Scores are 1 to 10. Repeat means how well an entry holds up as often as this moment happens. Overall is a judgement, not an average.

## Winner: 1c-pile-up

**Why.** Light with weight crumpling into a heap against Maya is the one idea that is funny, new and made of Lumen's own material, and it says exactly who was hit: the pile is on Maya and she swallows it. 1c tells it best for a TV. The hit-stop snap-zoom gets the 'oh!', the tall zigzag heap reads from the couch, and the gulp and blink in close-up is the punchline people will want again. Because the heap grows with range, it won't look the same every register.

**Porting.** This plays up to five times a round, so ration the camera. Snap-zoom only on a register with a single hit, or on the longest shot, and when several robots are hit at once, play every heap in parallel with no camera move (1a shows this works). Trim it to about 1.6-1.8 s. Keep heap size tied to range, so a point-blank shot makes a small curl, and give 2+ damage a bigger heap or a second gulp. Show the full beam from the shooter before the zoom, because the zoom crops the shooter (Theo is cut off at the frame edge). Remove the stray blue blob behind Maya during the gulp. Make a laser stopped by a wall end with no heap and no gulp, so the miss reads differently from a hit. Always ease the camera fully home.

## Runner-up: 3c-laser-pointer

**Why.** The laser pointer gets the biggest laugh in the set, because every cat owner on the couch sees the pounce coming. 3c's freeze-frame hangs Jun high in the air at the exact moment the beam lands on Maya, so the room has a beat to point and laugh while the actual hit stays clear in the held frame. It needs a bystander nearby, which keeps it an occasional treat rather than wallpaper.

**Porting.** Play it only when a robot that isn't involved stands within two spaces of the target, and rarely even then (for example once per bystander per game). Otherwise fall back to the winner. Fix the pac-man gap in Jun's ring during the crouch, and make the ghost an obvious faint trail of light so there are never two solid Juns. Check that Jun lands exactly on his space, facing his original direction. Lasers fire straight, so put the dot on Maya's row just short of her instead of off to the side, and hold the straight beam into her through the freeze. Skip the freeze and the tilted camera on registers where other hits need showing. 3a's orange blush is worth borrowing for Jun's guilty look.
