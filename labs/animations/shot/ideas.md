# Getting shot: 50 animation ideas (Lumen)

Theo (blue Prism, 5,1, facing west) fires down the row and hits Maya (red Pebble, 1,1, facing south) for 1 SPAM. Jun
(yellow Cube, 3,3) watches from two rows nearer us. This happens every register, so the budget is 2.5 s and nobody
moves. Every idea below ends with Maya at 1,1 facing south, Theo at 5,1 facing west and Jun untouched.

## Banned (too obvious)

- Sparks or shards bursting from the hit point (what the game does today)
- A red damage flash or glitch tint on the victim
- Camera shake on impact
- The victim knocked back a step, wobbling, then sliding home
- A generic spin or stagger of the victim
- Comic text: POW, ZAP, "-1", "+1 SPAM"
- A SPAM card icon popping up over her head
- An expanding shockwave ring or glow burst at the hit
- A glow charging up at the muzzle before the shot
- A bolt flying across like a bullet instead of a beam
- A puff of smoke or a scorch mark
- Hit-stop or generic slow motion on impact

## The ideas

1. **Edge-on.** The beam is a card of light seen exactly edge-on, so for the whole crossing it looks like an ordinary laser line. When it reaches Maya it turns 90° to face the camera, shows itself as the SPAM card (a thin ⚠), and slides into her like a slide into a projector. *Twist:* the laser was the damage card all along. You were seeing it from the side.

2. **Magnifying glass.** Theo is a Prism, so the white light from his eye passes through his body and comes out as a soft rainbow fan that sweeps the glass. The fan narrows to one hot pin-point on Maya's flank, and she gives a tiny hop, like a bare foot on a sunny pavement. *Twist:* his form does what real prisms do, and the shot becomes a kid with a magnifier and an ant. Each of the six forms could get its own shot like this.

3. **Under the ice.** The beam never crosses the air. It runs inside the slab like the belt streams, a blurred blue line moving under the smoked surface, and comes up right beneath Maya, lighting her from below like a face over a torch before it sinks away. *Twist:* Lumen's "light under the glass" becomes a sneak attack, and the light from below is spooky and funny.

4. **The only light in the room.** For one beat the room, the board and every glow go dark, and the beam is the only light left. So it casts real shadows: Maya's is flung long and thin westward off the slab into the darkness, and Jun's stretches toward us. Then the room comes back. *Twist:* a laser is a light, so in a world made of light it should throw shadows. The hit lands as a shadow torn out of her.

5. **Fly in a jar.** The beam goes into Maya's polished body and can't get out. A little blue spark bounces around inside her like a fly in a jar, pinging each wall with a tiny chime while she jiggles, until it escapes down through her base into the glass. *Twist:* total internal reflection, the real physics of glass, played as slapstick.

6. **Caustics.** The beam bends through Maya and pours into the slab under her. It spreads as a slow, wobbling net of blue caustics (the pattern sunlight makes on a pool floor), ripples a space or two out and fades. *Twist:* the loveliest thing light does in glass, spent on a bad thing. Getting hurt is gorgeous.

7. **Torch through fingers.** On impact Maya glows from inside, deep red and see-through like a hand held over a torch. For a moment you can see a little stack of slides inside her (her deck), and a dull junk slide drops onto it. *Twist:* light scattering through her becomes an X-ray of the rule. You see exactly where the SPAM goes.

8. **Bruise.** Where the blue beam meets her red polish, the colours mix into a bruise that blooms across her flank. It heals by drifting around the colour wheel (magenta, rose) back to her own red. *Twist:* damage as colour mixing, so the bruise's colour tells you who shot you. It stays rose-magenta, clear of Haywire's lilac.

9. **Fluorescent tube.** The hit knocks the light out of her. Her eye, ring and glow pool go out, and she is a dark shape, a hole in the board's light. Then she flickers back on in two stuttering blinks, tink-tink, like an office ceiling tube. *Twist:* in a world drawn with light, darkness is the injury, and everyone in the room has stood under that tube.

10. **Conservation of light.** Theo has to pay for his shot. The light in the glass drains toward him in a soft circle, seams and glows dimming as it pours into his eye. He fires, and after the hit the borrowed light spreads back across the board from Maya, like spilled water finding its level. *Twist:* the charge-up becomes bookkeeping. The board lends the light and gets it all back, so it ends exactly as bright as it began.

11. **Thunder after lightning.** The beam is instant and silent. Maya is hit and does nothing. Half a second later the chime arrives as a soft ripple crossing the glass from Theo, visibly slower than the light was, and only then does she flinch. *Twist:* light beats sound, and robots, like people, jump at the bang, not the flash.

12. **Ducked too soon.** Maya's eye flicks toward Theo and she squashes flat to duck, and the beam passes over her. She pops back up, relieved, straight into its tail end. *Twist:* the near miss that wasn't, which the rules always guaranteed. She ends standing exactly where she was.

13. **Hard to swallow.** The beam pushes a small lump of light into her. It travels down through her body like an egg down a snake, making her bulge as it goes, and she finishes with a single hiccup: a little bob and a high chime. *Twist:* SPAM clogs your deck, so she can barely get it down. The more SPAM she holds, the more she hiccups.

14. **Loose eye.** The impact knocks her eye light loose. It rolls around the front of her body like a marble in a bowl, wobbles, and clicks back into place facing south. *Twist:* her eye also shows which way she faces, so the gag ends by restating the rule: getting shot never turns you.

15. **Floating dock.** The slab floats in a dark room, so a hit at one end rocks it like a floating dock. The whole board tips a few degrees and back, Jun and Theo bob along, Maya's glow pool sloshes, and everything settles level. *Twist:* Lumen's floating slab taken literally. Nobody moves a space; everybody rides the wave.

16. **Pile-up.** The beam travels like a hose of light and doesn't stop on contact. It crumples against Maya's flank into folds and coils, piling up like a train into the buffers, and then the whole heap is slurped into her. *Twist:* light gets weight and momentum, and "the first robot in line stops the beam" comes true very literally.

17. **Zipline.** The beam stays lit, sloping slightly down from Theo's muzzle to Maya. A tiny glowing card hooks onto it, swings down the zipline into her with a ding, and the line pulls back into Theo. *Twist:* the laser is the delivery route for the junk mail.

18. **Tape measure.** The beam pays out like a carpenter's tape made of light, ticking off thin numbers 1, 2, 3, 4 at each space. It touches Maya on 4, then zips back into Theo's eye with a snap that makes him wobble. *Twist:* line of sight measured the way a war-gamer measures range, and everyone knows the snap of a tape measure going home.

19. **Side-eye.** After the hit Maya's body never turns, but her eye light slides around her edge toward Theo, gives him a long resentful look, and slides back to the front, facing south. *Twist:* a whole personality from one small light, and her facing is plainly unchanged.

20. **Gunslinger.** After firing, Theo's eye gives off a thin curl of warm-white light that rises and drifts like smoke from a barrel. His pointer spins one little holster twirl around his ring and stops pointing west. *Twist:* the Western cliché, built from Lumen's materials and kept tiny. Only the pointer twirls, so Theo never seems to turn.

21. **Sorry.** Theo dips forward in a small, apologetic bow with his eye dimmed. Maya dips a bow back, and both straighten. *Twist:* in a calm, polite world a laser hit is a courtesy, the opposite of what the room expects.

22. **Imagined comeback.** A faint glass ghost slides out of Maya, turns to face Theo and fires a wobbly dotted "thought" beam that fizzles halfway there. The ghost sags, turns back and sinks into the real Maya, who never moved. *Twist:* the robot's daydream of revenge as a see-through double. Its beam never reaches Theo, so the game stays honest.

23. **Courtside.** Jun, safe two rows nearer us, turns only his eye light to follow the beam from Theo to Maya like a tennis spectator. Then he slowly swings it round to face the camera and holds it there, deadpan. *Twist:* the bystander sells the hit with the sitcom look to camera, on a robot whose whole face is one light.

24. **The laser pointer.** The beam's spot first dances on the glass beside Maya like a laser-pointer dot. Jun's eye locks onto it, and he crouches and pounces half a space just as the dot jumps up onto Maya. Jun freezes mid-pounce, embarrassed, and slides back to his space facing north. *Twist:* every cat owner in the room knows this. Jun ends where he started, as if nothing happened.

25. **With compliments.** A ribbon of Theo-blue light ties itself around Maya with a little bow and a thin-type tag that reads "from: theo". Then the ribbon unties and sinks into her. *Twist:* SPAM as the present nobody wanted, polite and passive-aggressive.

26. **ow.** Tiny, calm, lowercase captions appear under the robots: Maya says "ow." and, a beat later, Theo says "my bad." *Twist:* the quietest possible reaction in a loud genre. The lines rotate ("rude.", "again?", "noted."), so the room starts waiting to read them.

27. **Riffle.** On impact a fan of thin glass slides (her deck) springs out above Maya. The SPAM slide slots into the middle and the fan riffles shut back into her with a soft shuffle. *Twist:* you watch the deck get clogged, and over a game the fan visibly thickens with dull junk slides.

28. **Back in the box.** For one beat Maya stops being Lumen: she becomes a flat grey plastic board-game piece with no light, no ring and no eye. Then light floods back into her from the hit point. *Twist:* the hit knocks her back into the cardboard box. The look breaks on purpose and comes home within half a second.

29. **Strings.** On impact, thin threads of light in her colour appear above Maya and rise out of the top of the frame toward the room. She is a marionette: the threads jerk and she twitches, then they lift away into the dark. *Twist:* the meta truth that someone on the couch is pulling her strings, with the threads leading toward the room.

30. **The rule, underlined.** The first time this happens in a game, a thin-type sentence runs along the beam like a news ticker: "the first robot in line takes 1 SPAM". It reads off as the beam crosses and fades with it. *Twist:* the rulebook is written on the laser itself, right where the rule happens. Later shots skip the caption.

31. **Glass harp.** The beam acts like a string. It is pulled taut, plucked at the moment of the hit, and visibly shivers in a standing wave as it rings one pitched glass note. *Twist:* it follows real string physics, so the pitch depends on the beam's length. Point-blank shots squeak, cross-board shots are cello notes, and the room learns to hear range.

32. **Register melody.** Each register's hits ring the next note of one five-note phrase, and Maya's glow pool lifts like a key on a glass xylophone as her note sounds. Over a round of shooting the hits quietly play a tune. *Twist:* the game's most frequent bad thing becomes its soundtrack, and everyone hums the phrase by the end of the night.

33. **Morse.** The beam fires in short and long pulses that spell S-P-A-M in Morse code (· · · / · – – · / · – / – –), each pulse a tiny chime, with the final long dash landing the hit. *Twist:* the laser literally sends the message, and the one person in the room who reads Morse will lose it. About 1.5 s.

34. **Skipping stone.** The light skips across the glass like a flat stone on a pond: four skips, one per space, each leaving a small ripple and a rising chime. The last skip taps Maya. *Twist:* the glass behaves like water, and the number of skips counts the range.

35. **Light, slowed.** Everything else freezes (robots, trails, the streams under the glass) and only light keeps moving. The beam crawls visibly across the four spaces with a bright head while Maya's eye inches toward it. Then time snaps back and the hit lands at full speed. *Twist:* bullet time turned inside out. Matter stops, light is the slow thing, and for once you watch light travel.

36. **Can't look.** At the moment of impact the camera turns its head away, a slow pan of about ten degrees off the hit. Then it peeks back at Maya, who is fine, and settles home. *Twist:* the camera has feelings, like the room covering their eyes. It is the opposite of a shake.

37. **Scope.** For rare shots (the first shot of the game), the camera drops to Theo's eye height behind him, and his ring of light grows into a crosshair circle that frames Maya down the row. The beam fires away from us, then the camera floats home. *Twist:* the crosshair is made from Theo's own ring of light, and for one beat the room is Theo.

38. **Wiped clean.** A sliver of the beam glances off Maya's polish straight at the camera and leaves a smear of blue light on the TV's own glass (an overlay). A soft swipe, like a thumb, wipes it off. *Twist:* the room's own screen is in the line of fire, and the gag stays gentle instead of the cracked-screen cliché.

39. **Delivery.** At the hit, a tiny slide of light leaves Maya and flies out of the bottom of the frame toward the HUD. At that instant her player's phone buzzes in their hand. *Twist:* the junk mail is delivered to the person on the couch, and the TV and the phone become one gesture.

40. **Afterimage.** After the beam is gone, a soft orange ghost of it floats over the picture (an overlay), drifting a little as it fades, exactly like the spot you see after looking at a bright light. *Twist:* a real afterimage in the opposite colour to Theo's blue, as if the TV's eyes got dazzled too.

41. **The audience.** At the moment of the hit, small lights like eyes open in the darkness around the slab, one in each player's colour. They turn toward Maya, blink and close. *Twist:* the dark room was never empty. It is the players, watching, and the people on the couch slowly realise it's them.

42. **Clogged.** For one beat the junk clogs the picture itself: the whole frame drops to big chunky pixels. Sharpness then sweeps back in from Theo's end to Maya's, along the beam's path. *Twist:* the look breaks on purpose. SPAM is junk, so the render clogs and then clears.

43. **The board insists.** Maya sidesteps half a space, the beam misses, and she settles smugly. Then the glass under her tilts, she slides back into the row, and the still-lit beam simply catches her. *Twist:* the game cheats, but only on the rules' side. She ends on her own space, facing south, hit exactly as written.

44. **Every time.** At milestones (her fifth SPAM of the game, say), faint ghosts of every beam that has hit her this game flash in together from wherever they came, all meeting at her for one breath, then fade. *Twist:* a chart of her bad luck drawn in light, the whole game's history of grudges in one picture.

45. **Dust in the beam.** The beam shows specks of dust drifting in the dark air along its length, the way a sunbeam does in a room. The hit stirs them into a slow swirl that drifts away. *Twist:* the room was always full of dust, and you only see it when light passes through, a small reward for watching closely.

46. **Kintsugi.** Where the beam hits, a fine seam of warm-white light traces across Maya's polish like a mended crack, glows, and fades out. *Twist:* damage shown as a beautiful repair, the Japanese art of mending with gold. Played in warm white, it is one of Lumen's own lights.

47. **Breath on glass.** The hit leaves a patch of mist on her polish, like breath on a cold window. A single tally stroke appears in it as if drawn with a finger, and then the mist clears. *Twist:* the glass behaves like glass, and the tally counts her SPAM for anyone looking closely.

48. **Heartbeat.** Maya's trail, which normally appears only when she moves, draws a quick jagged heartbeat spike on the glass under her, then two calm beats, then fades. *Twist:* Lumen's trail becomes a heart monitor, and from this camera the Pebble even looks like a heart. The calm beats say she's fine.

49. **Mirror, mirror.** In the glass's reflection under Maya, her mirrored self dodges the mirrored beam. A beat later the reflection notices, glitches, and snaps back into agreement with what really happened. *Twist:* a tiny detail for repeat viewers, in which the reflection lives a luckier life for half a second.

50. **Reaction deck.** Each robot holds a shuffled deck of a dozen small reactions (the side-eye, the hiccup, the loose eye, the bow, the fluorescent stutter...), and every hit draws the next one, so the same reaction never plays twice in a row. *Twist:* the game's own deck mechanic runs the animations, which keeps its most frequent moment fresh all night.
