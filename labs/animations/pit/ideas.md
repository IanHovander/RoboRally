# Falling into a pit: 50 animation ideas (Lumen)

Every idea starts with Maya's Move 3 (about 1 s, unless the idea changes it). Each one ends inside the 3.5 s budget with Maya gone (`on: false`), Theo still at (5,3) facing north, the pit back at (4,1), the camera home and every overlay cleared.

## Banned (too obvious)

- Shrink, spin and fade down the hole, then a red ring on the pit (what the game does today).
- Cartoon hang in mid-air, a look down (or at the camera), then the drop.
- A falling whistle, then a far-off crash or thud.
- Camera shake when she lands.
- A puff of smoke or dust, or sparks and debris shooting up out of the pit.
- A generic glow burst, flash or shockwave ring from the hole.
- Comic text pop-ups: "OOPS!", "-2", a big X, a skull, "K.O.".
- A swirling vortex or whirlpool drain that sucks her in.
- A ghost or soul floating up out of the pit, or a halo.
- Flailing, or scrabbling at the rim with little hands before slipping.
- A slow-motion replay, or the camera following her down the hole.
- A sad trombone, "wah wah wah".

## Ideas

1. **Coat check.** As Maya rolls onto the pit, her colour won't go in with her: the red light peels off at the rim and lies on the glass as a Pebble-shaped stain while her body drops in as plain dark glass. The abandoned red light shivers and drains away into the board's seams like water into grout. *Twist:* Lumen's own rule taken literally. A pit is where light is missing, so light can't go in, and she has to leave her colour at the door.
2. **The ring knows first.** Her ring of light only exists on the glass, so the moment she is over the hole it drops straight down like a hula hoop, and she hangs there ringless for a beat. She tips her eye down to see where it went, then plops in after it. *Twist:* the "look down" gag, except the one who notices the missing floor is the part of her that is drawn on the floor.
3. **Bridge of light.** She rolls out to the middle of the hole and doesn't fall: the trail she just laid holds her up like a glowing rope bridge from the rim to her base. Then, as trails do, it fades, sagging lower and lower, and she sinks with it until the last strand winks out. *Twist:* the trail's ordinary fade becomes the countdown to her fall, so the room can see exactly how long she has left.
4. **Rear-ended by her own trail.** She brakes hard at the brink and stops, safe, right on the rim. But her trail of light, which always lags a little behind her, keeps coming, bunches up like a pile-up of glowing cars, and bumps her in. *Twist:* in Lumen the trail always arrives a moment late, and here that delay is what pushes her in.
5. **Skid marks.** Seeing the hole, she slams on the brakes: her eye light swings round to her back as a brake light and her trail smears into two long skid marks of light. She slides, slower and slower, off the lip and in, and the skid marks fade with a hiss. *Twist:* a car-chase cliché built only from Lumen's parts, because an eye made of light and a trail of light are exactly what brake lights and skid marks are.
6. **Pour.** At the lip she softens and melts into liquid red light that pours over the edge like honey, the stream thinning to a thread. The last drop clings to the rim, stretches, lets go, and a moment later glints once, far below. *Twist:* the polished robot turns out to have been light all along, and in this room light pours like syrup.
7. **Heaviest first.** She falls apart in order of weight: the polished body drops like a stone, then the ring of light, then the pointer drifting down like a feather, and last her eye, a single red spark that sways down slowly and goes out halfway. *Twist:* light weighs nothing, so the lightest parts of a Lumen robot fall slowest. It reads as physics until you think about it.
8. **Blush.** The instant she vanishes, her red seeps out of the hole into the smoked glass, spreading through the slab like dye in water until the board's glowing edges flush pink. Then it clears, edges first, back to warm white. *Twist:* the glass itself is embarrassed for her. The slab blushes.
9. **Inhale.** As she reaches the hole, the light around it leans in: grid seams, the rim, her trail and even Theo's ring stretch toward the pit like smoke toward a chimney. The board breathes in with her, dims almost to black for a beat, then breathes out and the light settles back where it belongs, without her. *Twist:* an absence pulls. Instead of a spinning drain, the pit takes one slow breath, which no vortex does.
10. **Anti-checkpoint.** As she drops, a column rises out of the pit exactly like a checkpoint's column of light, except it is made of darkness, a tall black pillar that blots out the room's glow behind it. It stands for a beat, then sinks back into the hole like a lid closing. *Twist:* the pit "pays out" with the evil twin of the thing every player is racing toward.
11. **Narcissus.** The pit is a perfect black mirror: as she drives up, an upside-down Maya drives toward her from below its surface, eye to eye. They meet at the rim, touch noses, and the reflection pulls her through. *Twist:* she wasn't careless, she was curious. The reflective glass the whole look is built on is what lures her in.
12. **Lost marble.** She drops through the hole and out of the bottom of the slab, and through the smoked glass we see her as a blurred red glow landing on the dark floor below. She bounces twice and rolls off, her light slipping out of the board's spill into the dark. *Twist:* the most familiar fall there is, a marble rolling under the sofa, in a room we had forgotten has a floor.
13. **Falls up.** At the hole she rises instead of sinking, drifting up into the dark above the board and shrinking until only her eye is left. It settles there as one red star, twinkles once, and goes out. *Twist:* a pit is just where light is missing, and the dark room has no up or down, so she falls into the sky.
14. **Rim shot.** She clips the edge on the way down and the rim's thin line of light rings: a glow runs round and round the square, humming, like a wet finger on a wine glass. On the last beat Theo's ring gives one crisp flash: ba-dum, tss. *Twist:* a literal rim shot, played on the rim of the pit, with Theo on cymbal.
15. **Skipping stone.** Maya is a Pebble, so she skips: the darkness in the pit acts like still black water, and she bounces off it three times in little hops, each touch sending a soft ring of light across the black. On the third skip she is nearly at the far rim, then she sinks, like a stone. *Twist:* her form is a pebble and the pit is a pond for exactly three skips. She does everything right and still sinks, because stones do.
16. **Bonk.** Move 3 is fast, so she doesn't drop at the near rim: she flies across the hole and hits the far inner wall, leaving a Pebble-shaped dent of light in it. She hangs there, then slides down out of sight, and the dent fades. *Twist:* honest momentum. The pit's far wall is the punchline, and the glowing dent is the only evidence.
17. **Lip-in.** She rolls up to the hole like a putt at the end of its run, circles once around the rim, slows, and drops in with a hollow glass clunk. Across the board Theo's ring pulses three soft times, a golf clap. *Twist:* the golfer's lip-out in reverse, and the most polite reaction in sport from the robot who wasn't involved.
18. **Perfect ten.** At the brink she hops, tucks and dives in vertically, with no splash at all, because there is nothing to splash. Three small thin-type judges' cards rise along the rim, all reading 2. *Twist:* the judges' scores are her damage. Two damage, unanimously.
19. **Evel Knievel.** A ramp of light flicks up at the rim and Maya jumps the pit, landing on the far side. A beat later the pit quietly slides one space east under her, she drops, and the hole slides back to its own square, rim glinting innocently. *Twist:* she beats the pit, so the rules simply move the hole, and the board still ends exactly as it should.
20. **Look to camera.** As she drops, Theo slowly turns from facing north to face us, his eye brightening: a deadpan look into the documentary camera. A beat, then he turns back north as if nothing happened. *Twist:* the only witness shares the moment with the people on the sofa rather than with the board, and the room becomes his audience.
21. **Don't look in.** After she's gone, Theo turns his eye toward the pit and its light reaches out like a flashlight beam, but where the beam crosses the rim it gets eaten, shorter and shorter. Theo snaps it back like a hand off a hot stove and turns north again, a little faster than he turned away. *Twist:* not even light reaches the bottom of a pit. Theo learns this and pretends he never looked.
22. **Periscope.** She sinks slowly, leaving her pointer sticking up out of the hole like a periscope. It swivels around, spots the camera, pauses, then turns to point the way to the Reboot token, and slides under. *Twist:* her last act is useful. The little pointer that always shows which way she faces now shows where she'll come back.
23. **S.O.S.** The hole goes dark and quiet. Then, from deep inside, her eye blinks quick and tiny: dot dot dot, dash dash dash, dot dot dot, and goes out. *Twist:* only in the total dark of a pit could her little light be read as a signal, and repeat viewers start blinking along.
24. **Program cancelled.** The card she played hangs over the hole and its arrows tip from pointing forward to pointing down, then it drops in. Her remaining registers float up as small lit slides and tumble in after it, tink, tink, tink. *Twist:* the rule, "her program for the round is cancelled", made literal: her plans fall down the hole after her.
25. **One, two, three.** Each space she crosses lights a thin numeral of light on the glass, 1, 2. The 3 should land on the pit, but there is no glass to write it on, so it falls in after her, turning over as it goes, and the 1 and 2 fade. *Twist:* the card's count is written in light, and light needs glass to rest on, so the pit even eats the number.
26. **The rulebook.** As she drives, her path writes the rule along the glass in thin type, "A robot that moves onto a pit falls in", and when she drops, the last two words, "falls in", fall in. The rest of the sentence fades. *Twist:* the type acts out the rule, so the newcomer on the sofa learns it without anyone explaining.
27. **Back in the box.** As she disappears, a small glass game box slides into a corner of the screen with its lid open, and Maya lands in a velvet slot of light shaped like her, with a tiny clack. The lid closes and the box slides away. *Twist:* off the board means back in the box, as in any board game. The room is reminded it's a game, and that she'll be taken out again.
28. **Sliding puzzle.** After she drops, the board treats the pit as the gap in a sliding-tile puzzle: the neighbouring glass squares slide into the hole one after another, so the hole travels a small loop and clicks back into its own square. Every floor square is identical, so nothing has changed. *Twist:* the board tidies itself like a toy and ends exactly where it started. The only thing that moved was your expectation.
29. **The 3 made her do it.** She stops short at the brink, wobbling, refusing. A tall, thin "3" of light, the number from her card, floats up behind her and nudges her over with its curve, then fades. *Twist:* the program is the boss. The number on her card physically carries out the move she programmed.
30. **Telestrator.** The instant she drops, everything freezes and a sports analyst's pen of warm light draws on the screen: a loop round the pit, her path, and a dotted arrow showing where a Move 2 would have stopped her. The strokes wipe away and the game carries on. *Twist:* a sports-TV cliché drawn with Lumen's own lines of light, giving the room a post-match analysis of one bad decision.
31. **Missing key.** Each space she crosses lights up under her like a glass xylophone key, rising notes, one, two, three, and the pit is the next key in the scale, except it is missing. The phrase hangs unresolved in silence, until a low note answers from far down with a faint ring of light deep in the hole. *Twist:* the board is an instrument with one key missing, and the fall is the unresolved note the whole room waits to hear land.
32. **Siren song.** Each step chimes and sends a little ring of light out from her space, and the pit answers each time with a lower chime and a ring of darkness spreading from its rim, as if calling her in. On the third call and answer she goes to it. *Twist:* the move becomes a duet with the pit, and on repeat viewing you notice that the pit sang first.
33. **Sonar.** After she drops, soft rings of light rise out of the hole, one for each echo, every one fainter and later than the last, ping... ping...... ping........., until they stop. The spacing tells you how deep it goes: very. *Twist:* the depth of a bottomless pit shown as a rhythm you can see and hear.
34. **Going down.** As she sinks, the pit's four inner walls light up in bands that slide upward past her, faster and faster, like floors going by in an elevator shaft. A thin-type counter by the rim ticks B1, B2, B3 ... B∞, then a soft "ding", and it fades. *Twist:* deadpan civility. The disaster is delivered as an elevator ride, with a polite chime.
35. **Rewind.** At the brink everything freezes with her half over the hole, then time scrubs backward, her trail rolling up behind her to her start space. It replays at double speed exactly the same, and she still falls in. *Twist:* fate, or rather programming. The game gives her a do-over and her program plays out identically, because that is what programs do.
36. **Vertigo.** As she reaches the hole the camera does a slow dolly zoom: the board holds still while the pit seems to stretch deeper and deeper, its walls lengthening into a long shaft under the glass. She drops into it and the depth springs back to normal. *Twist:* Hitchcock's Vertigo shot, aimed at a single square of a party board game.
37. **Discretion.** Just as she tips, the camera politely pans away up into the dark, lingers for a second while we hear a soft clink, and pans back. The pit is just a pit and Maya is gone. *Twist:* not showing it is funnier than showing it, and the room imagines the fall for itself.
38. **Onto the lens.** For half a second the view cuts to underneath the board: the glass is a ceiling of soft light, the pit a black square, and Maya drops out of it straight at us, eye wide, until she hits the lens and the screen goes dark. Cut back to the calm board, without her. *Twist:* she falls onto the camera, so for a beat the TV itself is what she landed on.
39. **Long exposure.** As she falls, the screen holds a photographic long exposure: her whole move hangs as one smooth streak of red light from her start space to the rim, where it curls neatly into the hole like the flourish at the end of a signature. The photograph fades. *Twist:* Lumen's look is light-painting photography, and this is the shot it was always hinting at.
40. **Mind the gap.** A second after she has gone, a calm thin line of type fades in beside the pit, like a station announcement: "Please mind the gap." It fades out. *Twist:* the warning arrives late and perfectly calm, the most understated possible reaction to a disaster.
41. **Untitled (Absence).** As the hole settles, a small gallery label fades in beside it in thin type: "Maya. *Untitled (Absence)*, 2026. Light, glass, robot." *Twist:* the board, which Lumen half is anyway, is treated as a minimalist art installation, and the fall becomes conceptual art.
42. **Captions.** Closed captions appear at the bottom of the screen in thin type, describing the sound with documentary restraint: "[a small, dignified clunk]", then "[Theo pretends not to have seen]". *Twist:* the accessibility layer becomes a narrator, and the only commentary in a deliberately wordless look.
43. **Into your pocket.** After she drops, a thread of red light runs from the hole down the screen to her player chip in the HUD at the bottom, and the chip dims to show she's out. In the real game her player's phone glows red at the same moment. *Twist:* she falls out of the TV and into her player's pocket. The pit leads to the phone.
44. **Pits all the way down.** She drops through the hole and the camera tilts down just far enough to show, under the board, a smaller glass board with a pit in the same place, and another under that. She falls through one after another, smaller each time, until she's a dot, and the stack vanishes as the camera comes home. *Twist:* a bottomless pit made literal by recursion.
45. **Two timelines.** At the rim she splits: a pale copy of her rolls safely on past the hole while the real Maya drops in. The copy reaches its space, turns, notices it is see-through, looks at the hole, and fades away. *Twist:* the game shows the timeline where she programmed better, just long enough to sting.
46. **Portal.** She drops into the pit and instantly drops out of the top of the screen, back into the same pit, out of the top again, faster each loop, a red streak through the dark. On the third loop the top of the screen doesn't give her back. *Twist:* an endless fall, which the game ends simply by not returning her.
47. **Six ways down.** Each form falls in its own way, so the moment changes through the game: the Pebble skips and sinks, the Orb lips round the rim, the Prism wedges corner-down across the hole and then slips, the Capsule tips in like a pill into a bottle, the Cube fits the square hole exactly and slides down like a piston, and the Spire drops point-first like a dart. *Twist:* the six shapes finally have a reason to be different, and players start predicting how their friend will go.
48. **The pit gets bolder.** The pit gets a little bolder each time someone falls in: the first fall is a quiet drop, by the second its rim brightens as a robot approaches, and by the third the rim leans out a hair to meet its victim and flickers with satisfaction afterwards. It always ends back in its square. *Twist:* a running gag that rewards the players who have been watching all night.
49. **Name tag.** Her floating name tag, MAYA, stays hovering over the empty hole after she's gone. Then the letters notice and drop in one at a time, and the final A teeters on the rim before following. *Twist:* a detail you only catch on the third viewing: the last letter needs a moment to make up its mind.
50. **Theo's rainbow.** As she drops, the hole's rim catches the last of her light in one thin flare, and across the board Theo's prism body bends it into a small rainbow that fans across the glass at his base, then folds away. Theo doesn't move. *Twist:* refraction from the one Prism on the board, a quiet detail for repeat viewers that only happens when a Prism is watching.
