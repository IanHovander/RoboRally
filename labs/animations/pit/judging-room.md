# Pit: the room's verdict

I judged this as six friends on a couch watching the TV between turns. A fall into a pit happens a few times a game, so over a game night a room sees it something like 10 to 20 times. It is the classic Robo Rally disaster, and the room wants a reaction, not a set piece. I looked at every contact sheet and pulled frames from every video, at 10 to 30 fps around the key beats. Today's game (baseline) is a 2.2 s drive, sink and red ring. Every entry's moment runs 2.8 to 3.4 s, inside the 3.5 s budget.

Scores are 1 to 10. Overall is my judgement, not an average.

## Winner: 1b-show-off

This is the one the room will talk about. The ramp makes everyone lean in, the barrel roll and the pose to the sofa get a cheer, and the floor sliding out from under her mid-pose turns it into a howl, with Theo's double-take as a second laugh. Unlike 1a, the hole has a lit rim and slides visibly next to her body, so the cheat reads from the couch, and the hole tiptoeing home with a glint leaves no doubt that she is in the pit. Once the room knows the gag, the ramp popping up becomes the cue to shout.

**Porting:** The jump needs a legal landing space one past the pit. When that space is a wall, a robot, another pit or the board edge, or when she falls by push, belt or a Move 1 from the rim, fall back to a plain drop or a short hop to the rim. The fake hole and the glass patch must cover and restore whatever is on both cells (belts, gears), and both must be gone exactly when the moment ends. Keep the program card inside the HUD, since it clips off the top of the frame mid-jump. Give the reaction to the nearest robot only, and make sure it is back on its exact facing before the next beat (Theo is still turned at about 3.7 s). The moment is 3.33 s against a 3.5 s budget, so add nothing. Consider trimming the pose hold after the first fall of a game so the twentieth one stays snappy.

## Runner-up: 3a-pileup

This has the best mix of laugh, clarity and Lumen. The room watches her own trail wind up into a ball of light behind her while she sighs with relief, people point and shout 'behind you!', and the BAM with its shock ring lands the joke. It never leaves the wide board, follows her real path and ends exactly in the pit. It is built from the trail every robot already draws, so it feels like Lumen's own physics rather than a cartoon prop, and it is quick enough to hold up for many falls a night.

**Porting:** Use the robot's real trail points for the knot. Falls with no run-up (pushes, belts, Move 1) leave no trail to bunch up, so they need a fallback. She visibly stops one space short before being shoved in, so keep that stop brief in a busy register so nobody takes it for her final square. On a 10 to 13 space board the knot gets small, so keep it the brightest thing on screen. Make sure the teetering knot has faded before the next robot moves.

## Also worth keeping

- **2c-colour-field** is the best piece of Lumen in the set. If the team wants a quieter everyday fall, take its coat-check idea at game framing without the slow-down and the red room. 2b's scuttling stain is the lighter-hearted version.
- **4b-splat** is the scream of the night. It is too big for every fall, but it would be a good once-a-game treat, for example for the first fall of the evening.
- **5b-the-look** is the best running gag, because the witness changes by itself. It could be stacked on top of either winner whenever a robot stands near the pit.
