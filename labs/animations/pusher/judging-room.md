# Pusher: the room's verdict

Moment: a push panel on the north wall fires and shoves Maya one space south, toward the camera, still facing east.
How often: occasional, since only a couple of courses have push panels. The budget is 2 s. Today the game plays a
0.7 s plate shove.

This verdict judges the screen as six friends on a couch see it. It is based on each contact sheet plus frames
pulled from every video at 10–30 fps.

## Winner: 1c-against-the-glass

**Why.** This one makes the couch jump and then laugh: Maya flies at the TV and sticks to the inside of the screen
as her own outline drawn in light, her eye a bright bar and her pointer still aiming east, while a glint slides
across the glass. It is the purest Lumen version of the fourth-wall gag, because the robot is drawn on the last pane
of glass rather than smeared as paint. The pull-back, with her ghost shrinking into the one space she actually moved,
answers "where did she end up?" better than any other entry.

**Port: fix and watch.**
- Fix the single washed-out contact frame (around 1.2 s) where the camera passes into her 3D body. Hide the mesh a
  frame earlier, or cut straight to the silhouette.
- Trigger it from the push direction on screen, not the board's south. Panels that push any other way play another
  pick (the runner-up fits).
- The takeover hides the whole board for about 0.8 s. Play it only when nothing else in that register step needs
  watching. If she shoves a second robot, show that robot's move during the pull-back.
- Build the silhouette from each of the six forms and the player's colour. Keep the whole moment within the 2 s budget.

## Runner-up: 2b-rewind-take

**Why.** It gives the best gasp in the set: the camera drops low as the arm of light carries Maya over the slab's
edge, Ava lights up in horror, and the whole room thinks she's rebooting. Then the take reels back up her own trail
and settles on a ring at the true space, so the relief laugh lands and the rule (one space) ends up stated clearly.
It also works in any push direction, which makes it the natural partner for the winner's south-only gag.

**Port: fix and watch.**
- Overshoot only over empty floor, and only when an edge or pit is within two spaces. Otherwise play a plain shove.
- Never use it when a panel really does push a robot into a pit or off the board, because then the real fall must
  play.
- Keep the brink pause short, with no falling speck, so nobody reads it as a real fall.
- Limit the low camera swing: the labels disappear, and on a six-robot board other robots leave the frame.
- Tone down the white blow-out on the onlooker's gasp, and pick the nearest robot as the onlooker.
- Skip the overshoot when the push carries a second robot along.
