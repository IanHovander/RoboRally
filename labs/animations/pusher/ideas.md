# Pusher: 50 ideas for getting shoved by a push panel (Lumen)

## Banned (too obvious)

- A boxing glove or spring-loaded fist punching out of the wall
- The plate slamming out with a camera shake
- Sparks or a particle burst at the point of contact
- A comic "POW!" / "SHOVE!" word popping up
- Maya spinning or tumbling from the hit
- A white flash or glow burst on the panel
- A shockwave ring blasting out from the panel
- Speed lines or motion streaks behind her
- A rubbery squash-and-stretch bounce on impact
- Dizzy stars circling over her afterwards
- Skid marks or a dust puff where she stops
- A slow-motion instant replay

## 50 ideas

1. **The door left ajar.** The wall fin behind the panel opens a crack and a wedge of warm light spills across the glass, motes drifting in it like dust in a sunbeam; the wedge widens and sweeps Maya one space south like a leaf in a draught, then the crack eases shut. *Twist:* there is no plate at all. The shove is made of Lumen's one material, and for a beat the dark room seems to have a brighter room behind its wall.
2. **Breath on the glass.** The panel exhales and a bloom of frosty condensation spreads across the glass from the wall. Maya skates one space south over it, and as the fog evaporates from its edges inward, a clear Pebble-shaped patch lingers where she stood. *Twist:* a crime-scene outline drawn in breath, gone within the second.
3. **The pool cue.** A hair-thin line of light slides out of the panel and lines up on Maya's back, with the fussy waggles and draw-backs of someone setting up a match-winning shot, and then gives her the gentlest possible kiss so she rolls exactly one space. *Twist:* snooker-final concentration for a one-space nudge; a speck of "chalk" light stays on her back and fades.
4. **Ding-dong.** The panel chimes a polite two-note glass doorbell. Maya's eye-light slides round her body to the north side to see who's calling, the plate shoves her squarely in the face, and as she settles her eye slides back to the front. *Twist:* politeness, then rudeness. Only her eye looked and her pointer never moved, so the room also sees that she still faces east.
5. **Spending the register.** This register's lit dot pops out of the panel's face as a bead of warm light, drops to the glass, rolls into Maya and bonks her one space south, then rolls back up the wall and clicks into its socket. *Twist:* the panel literally spends the register printed on it, so the room learns that the dots on a panel mean something.
6. **Newton's cradle.** Three small glass beads hang on threads of light in a row between the plate and Maya. The plate taps the first bead, a click travels through the middle two, and Maya swings out one space as the last ball; she starts to swing back as physics demands, catches herself, and stays. *Twist:* she refuses Newton, because the rules said one space and only one.
7. **The plunger.** The panel draws back like a pinball plunger, a coil of light winding tighter with a rising chime while Maya wobbles nervously. The release is enormous, and she stops dead after exactly one space as though she hit an invisible pane, quivering. *Twist:* all that windup, and the rulebook is the brake.
8. **A one-tile belt.** The panel sends a single pulse into the streams under the glass: a short, bright river flows south beneath her space and the next, she rides it like a conveyor, and it drains away the moment she arrives. *Twist:* it borrows the belt's own language, so anyone who has seen a belt reads "carried one space south" at once. It is a conveyor belt that lives for one second.
9. **Her reflection goes first.** Maya's reflection in the smoked glass is shoved on its own and slides a space south while she stays put; a beat later she notices and scoots after it until she is lined up over herself again. *Twist:* the reflection took the hit, and the robot is only catching up with it.
10. **Skipping stone.** She is a Pebble, so she skips: three quick, shrinking hops inside the one space, each touchdown sending a soft pond ripple of light across the glass, and the last one settles her dead centre on her new space. *Twist:* the robot's form decides the gag, and for a second the glass slab behaves like a pond.
11. **Curling.** The plate releases her like a curling stone. Two tiny streaks of warm light sweep frantically just ahead of her as she glides, and a faint bullseye of rings blooms around the next space just as she stops dead in its centre. *Twist:* the Pebble is exactly the shape of a curling stone, and the joke is the frantic sweepers against her serene glide.
12. **Gesundheit.** The panel's dots dim and its plate retracts in a hitching "ah... ah..." (the dots flicker with each catch of breath), then it sneezes a fine spray of warm motes that blows Maya one space; Ava's eye-light glances over. *Twist:* the wall has allergies, and the bystander's glance is the "bless you".
13. **The force arrives late.** The plate punches and nothing happens, so Maya's eye-light looks left, then right. Then a slow ripple of refraction, visibly crawling through the glass from the wall like heat haze, reaches her and nudges her one space (0.2 s punch, 0.5 s nothing, 0.6 s ripple, 0.4 s slide). *Twist:* the push travels at the speed of comic timing.
14. **Ava stifles a laugh.** The push itself plays plainly, but across the board Ava's ring flickers in three quick little pulses as her body bobs in a stifled giggle, with one last snort-pulse as Maya's trail fades. *Twist:* the bystander mirrors the room's own reaction, and Ava's player gets elbowed on the couch.
15. **Ava's alibi.** A split second before the panel fires, Ava's eye-light flicks toward it as though she just pressed a button; afterwards she rolls a couple of degrees away and back, the picture of innocence, still facing west. *Twist:* it suggests a culprit where there is none, and the room accuses Ava's player instantly.
16. **Tilt the table.** The panel kicks the slab instead of the robot: the whole glass board tips a few degrees toward us, Maya slides one space downhill, Ava's ring flares as she grips the glass right at the south edge, and the slab levels again. *Twist:* the push is staged as gravity, and the punchline is Ava hanging on beside the drop while only Maya slides.
17. **The drawer.** A thin glass drawer slides out of the wall under Maya's space and carries her one space south, like a till drawer opening with a bright chime, then whips back into the wall so fast that she is left sitting on her new space. *Twist:* two physics gags in two seconds. She is served rather than shoved, and it ends on the tablecloth trick.
18. **The step sequencer.** A playhead of light runs across the panel's five dots in a quick glass tick-tick-tick, passing the unlit dots with a muted tick, and the plate fires exactly as the playhead crosses this register's lit dot. *Twist:* the panel's printed schedule becomes a rhythm, so the room learns "this one fires on 1, 3 and 5" without a word.
19. **The panel's tune.** Each push panel firing strikes one glass note, with its registers 1, 3 and 5 tuned to do, mi and sol, and the dot that fires rings with a halo that shimmers faster for higher notes. *Twist:* the payoff spans the round. A panel that fires on all three of its registers plays a full chord by the end, and ears start to know where in the round they are.
20. **Twang.** The wall fin above the panel is a plucked string: the shove is the pluck, and the fin keeps vibrating as a blurred double line of light that hums down to stillness exactly as Maya comes to rest. *Twist:* Lumen's walls are bright fins, so of course they sing, and the slowing blur doubles as a timer for the slide.
21. **Maya rings.** The plate strikes her like a clapper striking a glass bell. Her polished body shivers with a fast, tiny tremble and her ring of light pulses at the bell's tone, the pulses slowing and fading as she slides to rest. *Twist:* she is a glass object, so being hit makes music, and the decay follows real bell physics rather than a cartoon bounce.
22. **Pressed against the TV.** She is shoved toward us, so the camera snaps in on her as she slides, and for a beat a soft red smear of her polish and eye-light squashes against the inside of the TV screen (an overlay) with a glass squeak. The camera pulls back and she has moved one space. *Twist:* breaking the fourth wall makes it feel enormous, and the pull-back shows how small it really was.
23. **Cuckoo.** The panel is a clock door: on its register a tiny bird drawn in three strokes of warm light pops out on a stick, sings "cu-ckoo" in two glass notes, and pokes Maya one space south with its beak before springing back as the door shuts. *Twist:* push panels fire on a schedule, so a cuckoo clock is the exact metaphor. On registers 3 and 5 it cuckoos the register number, faster each time, and only the last cuckoo pokes.
24. **Hiccups.** The panel has hiccups: three half-hearted shoves, each nudging her a third of a space with a tiny "hic" chime and a jolt of the dots, the last one slightly apologetic. *Twist:* one space delivered in instalments, landing exactly on the right space after the third hic.
25. **The dodge.** Maya sees the panel's dot light and hops one space south on her own; the plate punches empty air at full stretch, holds there, and droops sheepishly back into the wall. *Twist:* she "dodges" into exactly the space the push would have put her in, so the rules hold and her dignity is intact.
26. **Tiddlywink.** A soft fingertip of light reaches out of the panel and flicks the rim of the Pebble; she pops up in a little arc and lands in the next space with a glassy "plink", wobbling flat. *Twist:* for once we glimpse the board-game player's hand under the glass, flicking a token.
27. **The wall plays a card.** A lit program card (the phone's glowing slide) showing a south arrow slides out of the panel's slot, flies over Maya and drops into her like a card into a slot. She carries it out: one space south, still facing east. *Twist:* the wall programs you, in the phone's own visual language, so every player reads it at once.
28. **Lights out.** The panel is a light switch: it clicks and the whole room drops to black for half a second, leaving only the panel's dots and Maya's trail drawing itself one space south in the dark; the light returns and she is there, eye-light blinking. *Twist:* the move happens offstage and is shown only as Lumen's trail, which is all the evidence anyone needs.
29. **Chronophotograph.** Her slide is lit by four quick strobes, each leaving a fading ghost of her in light, so for an instant four Mayas step down the space like a Muybridge plate. *Twist:* the trail becomes a stack of still frames instead of a smear.
30. **Refracted path.** The panel's warm light enters Maya's polished back, bends through her and comes out of her south side as a red stripe on the glass that reaches exactly one space; she slides into her own light. *Twist:* the push passes through her before it moves her, and the robot turns out to be a lens.
31. **Slosh.** When she is shoved, Maya's inner glow lags behind and sloshes to her north side like liquid in a jar, then slops against her south side as she stops, and settles level. *Twist:* she is a glass vessel full of light, and her own glow makes inertia visible.
32. **Exchange of light.** At contact the panel's warm white floods into Maya, so she flashes white-hot while the plate blushes Maya's red; as she slides they trade back, and each is home in its own colour by the time she stops. *Twist:* the push is shown as a transfer of light, with the panel wearing her colour like a fingerprint for half a second.
33. **The bubble.** The panel blows a glass bubble that swells between the plate and Maya, pressing her one space south, then pops into a fine shimmer of glass dust that drifts down onto the board. *Twist:* the gentlest force imaginable, with the most satisfying pop.
34. **Zen garden.** The shove rakes the glass: as she slides, fine concentric arcs of light comb out around her like a raked zen garden, framing her calmly on her new space before they fade. *Twist:* a shove made serene, in keeping with Lumen's calm.
35. **Blueshift.** As she slides toward the camera, her red shifts slightly toward violet, then snaps back to red the moment she stops. *Twist:* a physics joke for repeat viewers: coming toward us, she blueshifts.
36. **Exclamation mark.** Her trail from the shove is a short vertical streak that breaks off just behind her, so from the couch the streak and her glowing ring read as a "!" before it fades. *Twist:* a push straight toward the camera is the one move whose trail can draw a perfect exclamation mark, and Lumen lets it.
37. **Overshoot, undo.** The plate shoots out on a telescoping arm far too long, carrying her two extra spaces to the brink of the south edge, where she teeters; then the game "undoes": she is reeled back up her own trail to the right space and the arm retracts sheepishly (0.6 s out, 0.3 s teeter, 0.6 s reel-in). *Twist:* absurd overreach, with the trail acting as the board's undo history, and she ends exactly where the rules say.
38. **Immovable.** The plate presses and nothing happens. Maya looks around, oblivious, while the plate strains and quivers; then the panel's other lit dots flare to lend their strength, together they shove her one space, and the dots dim back to normal. *Twist:* the panel borrows strength from its own future registers.
39. **Back-talk.** After the shove, Maya indignantly creeps half a space back north toward the panel. The plate twitches out an inch in warning, and she reverses onto her new space and settles with a little slumping sigh. *Twist:* an argument the rules win, and she ends exactly in the rules' space.
40. **Eye left behind.** When she is shoved, her eye-light hangs in the air over her old space for a beat, blinks, then zips after her and pops back into her front. *Twist:* the old cartoon gag done with Lumen's eye, which is only a light, so it really can be left behind.
41. **The compass needle.** The shove knocks her body thirty degrees off true, but her pointer on the glass stays rigidly east like a compass needle, and her body swings back into line with it as she lands. *Twist:* it shows the rule "pushed robots keep their facing" as physics instead of text.
42. **The crack that isn't.** On impact, bright fracture lines spiderweb across Maya's polish and the room gasps (has she shattered?); then, as she slides, the lines slip off her like light off a turning glass, because they were only reflections of the plate. *Twist:* a push does no damage, and the fake-out proves it.
43. **Judges.** Shoved, she glides with a full figure-skater's pirouette that ends facing east again, and three scorecards in thin type flip up at the bottom of the screen: 5.8, 5.9, 6.0. *Twist:* the banned spin, transformed by its judges; the room becomes the judging panel.
44. **Cardboard.** For one beat the light goes out of the world: the board becomes flat printed cardboard, Maya a cardboard standee, and a flat cartoon arm nudges her one square. Then the glass and light come back, with her exactly there. *Twist:* it shows the board game under the glass, a deliberate break from the look that comes straight back to it.
45. **Abacus.** A rod of light runs down her column; Maya slides along it like an abacus bead and clacks against a stopper of light one space down, and the rod fades. *Twist:* the grid becomes a counting frame, and the clack is pure satisfaction.
46. **Time debt.** At contact, time slows to a crawl and her eye-light swells in horror; then the scene fast-forwards at triple speed to pay back the time it borrowed, everything zipping into place exactly on schedule. *Twist:* the slow motion isn't the joke; paying it back is.
47. **Next stop, register 3.** After firing, the spent dot dims and a small bead of light walks along the panel's row of dots, like a clock hand, to the next lit one, where it waits for a second before fading. *Twist:* a useful tell disguised as a flourish, so everyone now knows when this panel strikes again. After register 5 it walks all the way round to 1, for next round.
48. **Trophy notches.** After the shove, a tiny pip in Maya's red is etched into the top edge of the panel's housing, so over a game each panel collects the colours of everyone it has pushed. *Twist:* a detail that pays off only across a whole game, as one panel slowly reveals itself as the bully of the board.
49. **Through the lens.** For a beat, the glass between the panel and Maya bulges into a magnifying lens, so the little plate looms hugely enlarged and terrifying as it comes; the lens relaxes and the actual shove is tiny. *Twist:* refraction used for comic scale: a monster that turns out to be a doorstop.
50. **Fingerprint.** The plate leaves a misty print of itself on Maya's polished back; once she stops, she gives a quick shimmy and a highlight sweeps across her body, wiping it clean. *Twist:* a fastidious robot, and a detail most people won't notice the first time.

## The five to animate

1. **22. Pressed against the TV.** The biggest laugh in the set: the TV's glass becomes the last pane in Lumen, and because few panels push toward the camera, the big camera move stays rare.
2. **37. Overshoot, undo.** A gasp at real stakes (she is about to go off the edge) turns into a laugh when the board rewinds her up her own trail of light.
3. **23. Cuckoo.** A clockwork character the room counts along with; the cuckoos match the register number, so repeats become a running gag and teach the panel's 1-3-5 timetable.
4. **17. The drawer.** A sleight of hand made of glass: she is served on a drawer that slides out of the wall, and then the tablecloth trick leaves her behind, which people will want to see again.
5. **1. The door left ajar.** The calm note: a wedge of warm light from a brighter room behind the wall carries her like a leaf, a moment of wonder that reads at any size.
