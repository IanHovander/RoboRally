# Drive: the room's verdict

I judged as six friends on a couch watching the TV between turns. The moment: Maya runs four ordinary cards, one
per beat: Move 2, Rotate Right, Move Back, U-Turn. This is the most common moment in the game: every robot, every
register, hundreds of times a night. The budget is 4.5 s. Today's plain version (baseline) takes 3.03 s.

The entries fall into three groups, and I judged each by how often it would actually play:

- **Every card, every time:** the ribbons (4b, 4c) and the steering cards (5a, 5b, 5c). These have to stay quick
  and quiet.
- **A narrow trigger, a few times a game:** "caught watching" (1a, 1b, 1c). The full gag plays only when a robot
  facing the camera plays Move Back. A short take plays, with no added time, whenever a robot turns to face us.
- **Rare by design, once or twice a game:** the claw machines (2a, 2b, 2c) and the dolphins (3a, 3b, 3c).

4a-ribbon has only a preview render and no video, so I didn't judge it.

I judged from the contact sheets plus my own frame pulls from every video: full 10 fps strips, 30 fps runs over
the key beats, full-frame stills, and a downscaled side-by-side of the three claws to check what reads at TV
distance.

## Winner: 2b-claw-machine (The claw)

**Why.** The claw is the moment the couch shouts "again!". Everyone knows an arcade claw, so the second it lowers
out of the dark the whole room is in on the joke, and the wobble over the spot is real suspense: arcade claws drop
things. 2b is the clearest claw of the three, a classic three-pronged shape. Maya's reactions (the look up and gulp,
the dangle, the glare, the nose-in-the-air U-Turn) turn it into a tiny story without moving the camera. It also
follows the rule to the letter: one space back, still facing south, ending exactly on (3, 1) at 4.13 s, inside the
4.5 s budget.

**Port: what to fix or watch.**
- **Theo's look-up.** It tips his prism into the glass so it reads as broken. Drop it, or limit bystanders to a
  brief turn. Better still, keep other robots still, because their facing is game state the room reads.
- **Rarity.** Keep it once or twice a game, seeded, and only on a Move Back with clear air above and an empty
  destination. Never give it to the same player twice in a row.
- **Borrow from 2a.** Take the pendulum swing on the carry and the pink shock ring on landing. The ring gives the
  drop a punchline that reads from the back of the room.
- **Light.** Keep the claw in Lumen's warm white, and check the bloom doesn't wash out the robot's colour while
  she's gripped.
- **Trail and register.** Draw no trail for the carried space, as now. If several robots play Move Back in the
  same register, only one gets the claw. Keep the Move Back card visible throughout, as now, since it carries the
  rule.

## Runner-up: 2a-claw-machine (Claw machine)

**Why.** The same laugh with more weight. The claw bounces on its line and strains as she comes off the glass, she
swings under it like a real pendulum, and the drop rings the glass with a shock ring that reads from anywhere in
the room. 2b has the better-acting robot and 2a the better-feeling machine. Either would get the "again!".

**Port: what to fix or watch.**
- **The slip.** Make the slip before the drop readable (a visible drop of a few pixels, then the catch), or cut
  it. At TV size it doesn't show.
- **Brightness.** Pull the claw from white-hot to Lumen's warm white and cut the bloom, so Maya's red stays visible
  in the grip.
- **Reel-up.** Keep the fingers rigid as the claw reels up. Right now they flail into a squiggle.
- **Time.** At 4.40 s it's right at the 4.5 s budget, so trim the hover rather than the drop.

## Also worth porting: 4c-light-painted-ribbon

The claw is a rare treat, so the everyday look of driving stays as it is today. If the team wants something on
every card as well, 4c is the one that will hold up hundreds of times a night. It's pure Lumen, adds no time, and
leaves "she turned here" on the glass. Fix one thing first: when the curl is tugged straight, lay the ribbon along
the cells she actually drove (a right angle), not on a diagonal.
