# Drive: fifty ideas for everyday driving and turning (Lumen)

Maya runs Move 2, Rotate Right, Move Back, U-Turn and must end on (3, 1) facing north, within 4.5 s. This happens to
every robot on every register, so most ideas are small enough to watch hundreds of times; the loud ones are marked
*(rare)* and are meant to fire once or twice a game, using the second or so of slack in the budget.

## Banned (too obvious)

- Speed lines or motion-blur streaks behind a moving robot.
- Dust puffs or skid smoke at the start and stop of a move.
- A cartoon hop with squash and stretch on every space.
- A whoosh spin with a swirl or vortex for turns.
- A follow-cam or zoom-in on whoever is moving.
- A glow burst or flash ring every time a robot arrives.
- The squares of the path lighting up, or footprints on each space.
- The truck "beep beep" and flashing hazard lights on Move Back.
- Blinking turn signals before Rotate Left or Right.
- A ghost preview of the path, or onion-skin afterimages along it.
- Leaning into turns like a drifting car, with a tyre squeal.
- A wind-up rock backward before every move.

## Ideas

1. **Over the shoulder.** On Move Back, Maya's eye light slides around her polished body to the rear, as if she's looking behind her, rides there for the one space back, then glides home to her front as she stops. *Twist:* every driver's reversing glance, made only of light moving across glass; the pointer never moves, so "backward, without turning" stays perfectly legible.
2. **Blink on the turn.** As each turn starts, the eye squeezes to a thin line for a couple of frames and opens on the new heading; the U-Turn gets one slightly longer blink. *Twist:* people really do blink when they turn their heads; nobody will name it, but the robots suddenly read as alive, and it costs no time at all.
3. **Reading the card.** When a card pops up above Maya, her eye tips up to it for a tenth of a second, then back down to the job. *Twist:* the robots visibly read the instructions the players wrote, so every misprogrammed disaster later in the game feels like the player's fault, not the robot's.
4. **Six gaits.** Each form drives its own way and leaves its own trail: Pebble glides and slows like a curling stone (a smooth line), Orb rolls with its highlight turning over (a fine wave), Cube tips edge over edge (dots where it touched), Capsule skates like a puck (twin rails), Prism hops facet to facet (dashes), Spire leans into the move and swings upright at the stop (a line that thickens where it braked). *Twist:* the forms stop being skins and become characters, and the trail textures tell robots apart even for colour-blind players.
5. **Driving signature.** Each player's robot keeps one driving style all game, seeded from their seat: one pulls away a hair late and brakes crisply, one drifts in a faint S between spaces, one eases in like a limousine. *Twist:* after a few rounds the room says "that is so Theo" about a robot moving one space; the variety belongs to people, not to randomness, so it never feels noisy.
6. **Swagger and nerves.** Body language follows the standings: the leader drives a touch slower and settles with a lazy lean back, while the robot in last place drives quick and low and checks its pointer with a tiny twitch at each stop. *Twist:* the most common moment in the game quietly tells the story of the race, and it changes as the race changes, so it cannot get stale.
7. **The audience in the corner.** Theo, idle at the top of the board, watches: his eye slides across his facets to follow Maya as she drives, and when her U-Turn swings her round toward his side of the board he looks away politely. *Twist:* idle robots become spectators, so the board is alive even when only one robot moves, and it adds no time.
8. **Contagious turn.** When Maya U-Turns, Theo gives a tiny involuntary twitch of a turn, like catching someone else's yawn, then straightens up as if nothing happened. *Twist:* the kind of detail people only notice on the fifth viewing, and then watch for every time.
9. **Double take.** The U-Turn hangs for a beat at the halfway point, Maya side-on as if she's glimpsed something, then she finishes the second quarter faster. *Twist:* a 180° turn becomes "huh? ...oh!" in one gesture; the hitch is under a tenth of a second, so it fits inside the turn's beat.
10. **Bartender's slide.** Moves aren't driven, they're slid: a quick shove of light at her tail, then Maya coasts across the glass with real friction and stops dead centre on her space with a tiny wobble, like a glass sent down a bar. *Twist:* the precision is the joke; it always lands exactly on the mark, as if a master bartender were playing the game.
11. **Balloon on a string.** The card above Maya floats like a balloon tied to her: it lags when she pulls away, swings past her when she stops, and swings round after her on turns. *Twist:* the label becomes a toy without changing what it tells you, a free bit of follow-through on every move that makes the robot's speed easy to feel.
12. **Pivot foot.** Rotate Right is a basketball pivot: Maya plants her nose and swings her tail around it, then settles back to the centre of her space. *Twist:* a sports move instead of a spin; because the tail swings out on the opposite side, right and left turns have different silhouettes from across the room.
13. **Three-point turn in a box.** The U-Turn is done like a careful car: a little arc forward-left, a back-up to the right, a forward nudge, all inside her own square, ending facing north. *Twist:* it obeys "in place" to the letter (she never leaves her space) while poking fun at robots that just spin; here it caps a forward, turn, reverse, turn program that is already a parking manoeuvre.
14. **Elastic (rare).** Once or twice a game, Move 2 carries Maya a third of a space too far; her trail goes taut behind her like a rubber band and twangs her back to space two with a shiver of light along its length. *Twist:* the rules are enforced physically, by her own trail; rare enough to stay a surprise, and it still finishes inside the move's beat.
15. **False start (rare).** Every so often, on Rotate Right, Maya starts to turn left; the card above her taps down on her with a pulse of light, and she swings the right way. *Twist:* the program is the boss, and a robot being corrected by its own card is the joke; she still lands on the right heading before the next beat.
16. **The long way round (rare).** Once in a while a Rotate Right is played as one smooth three-quarter turn to the left, landing on exactly the same heading. *Twist:* the room shouts "wrong way!" for half a second before realising it's legal; pure rule-bending that changes nothing in the game.
17. **Liquid eye.** The eye light behaves like a drop of liquid inside the glass body: it lags back as Maya pulls away, sloshes forward when she stops, swirls once on a turn, then settles. *Twist:* acceleration lives inside the material instead of in dust or bounces; the robot stays composed while its light gets jostled.
18. **Long exposure.** A turn leaves a faint arc swept by the pointer around the ring, like a long-exposure photo of a turning lamp: a quarter arc for Rotate Right, a half circle for the U-Turn, fading with the trail. *Twist:* Lumen's trail finally records turns as well as moves, so someone who looks up late can read the whole program off the glass.
19. **Negative trail.** Driving backward draws a trail of darkness instead of light: a soft line where the glass's own glow is wiped away, which lightens back within a second. *Twist:* reverse is literally the negative; a Move Back reads from across the room without an icon, borrowing the pits' "absence of light" without lasting long enough to be mistaken for one.
20. **Reluctant reflection.** Maya's reflection in the smoked glass (a dim mirrored copy just below the surface) follows a few frames late and catches up with a soft overshoot at each stop; on the U-Turn it turns the other way round to meet her. *Twist:* the twin in the glass has all the physics while the robot obeys the rules exactly; spinning the wrong way is the giveaway that the reflection has a mind of its own.
21. **Smoke in the glass.** The smoke in the smoked glass is real: slow curls of it, lit faintly in Maya's red, eddy in her wake beneath the surface and spiral where she turned, settling back to dark over two seconds. *Twist:* the material's name becomes a physical thing, and the lingering curls show where she just was, deeper and softer than the trail.
22. **Warm glass.** When a robot stops, a faint frosted patch the shape of its base fogs the glass beneath it, like a warm glass set on a cold table; when it drives off, the patch stays behind and clears. *Twist:* every move leaves a breath-mark footprint that evaporates, and idle robots slowly frost a full patch, a little clock of how long they've sat still.
23. **Lighthouse eye.** During a turn, the eye throws a faint cone of warm light across the glass that sweeps round with her; when it crosses Theo, his polished facets glint for an instant. *Twist:* turning becomes looking around the room, and bystanders catch the light like a face catching a passing torch.
24. **Meniscus.** The glass dimples under every robot like water under surface tension, bending the grid lines near it; the dimple travels with Maya and wobbles flat behind her. *Twist:* weight shown by refraction, light bending, rather than by a bounce; it's the glass being glass.
25. **Ribbon.** The trail behaves like a ribbon tied to Maya's tail: Rotate Right twists it into a small curl at her feet, the U-Turn ties a full loop, and her next move pulls the curl straight before it fades. *Twist:* the turn history becomes a knot you can read, physical comedy made of the trail itself.
26. **Drink the trail.** Once her last card is done and the trail has had its moment, it flows back along the glass into Maya's ring like a drink up a straw, and her body brightens a touch as it arrives. *Twist:* trails aren't just fading, they're reabsorbed, so each program reads as a breath out and a breath in, and the glass is clean for the next robot.
27. **Magnetic travel set.** In the last tenth of each move Maya is pulled into the centre of her space and lands with a tiny click, like a piece on a magnetic travel chess set; her ring of light contracts the last hair onto the space on the click. *Twist:* the exact space reads instantly, and the snap is a board game's tactile pleasure translated into light.
28. **Fingertips.** The glass is a touchscreen: a soft fingertip of light in the player's colour pushes the robot for a move, draws it back for Move Back, and two fingertips twist it for a turn, then lift away. *Twist:* the players' phones become invisible hands on the TV, a reminder that these are their pieces, moved by their choices.
29. **Thrown card.** Each card arrives from the bottom edge of the screen, where the room's phones are, flicked up like a card dealt across a table, and spins to a stop above Maya. *Twist:* it ties the hands in the room to the TV; the card visibly leaves "your hand" and lands on your robot.
30. **Spent card.** The card's light pours down into the robot as the card plays, draining top to bottom like an hourglass, and it is empty exactly when the move ends. *Twist:* the card becomes a progress bar nobody has to read, and you can see at a glance which instruction is being used up.
31. **Steering-wheel card.** The card above Maya isn't a label, it's the controls: for turns it rotates in its own plane like a steering wheel, for moves it tips forward like a throttle, and back for Move Back. *Twist:* the card moves a split second before the robot, so instruction and execution read as one gesture, like watching a driver's hands.
32. **Annotator.** When a program looks odd, a thin chess annotation appears beside the robot as it finishes: "?!" for driving forward and then backing up, "!" for a tidy park, fading within a second. *Twist:* chess notation's dry judgement applied to party robots; Maya's Move 2 followed by Move Back earns a deadpan "?!".
33. **Trick names (rare).** When a program happens to form a manoeuvre, its name surfaces in thin type under the robot like a skateboard trick: forward, right, back, about-face is "parallel park"; two U-Turns in a row is "pirouette". *Twist:* players start building programs to earn the names, a secret achievement system that changes no rules.
34. **Claw machine (rare).** Once in a long while, Move Back is done by an arcade claw of light lowered from the dark: it grips Maya, lifts her one space back, still facing the same way, and drops her with a little bounce. *Twist:* exactly the rule ("back one space, without turning") performed by the silliest possible machine; it spends the spare second of the budget and the look is home by the next card.
35. **Frets.** The grid lines are strings: as a robot crosses one it plucks it, and the line shimmers for a moment with a glass note; forward moves climb in pitch, Move Back steps down. *Twist:* every program plays its own little phrase, so the room learns to hear a Move 3 before looking up, and the shimmering line is all the picture it needs.
36. **Rubbed rim.** Turning in place is a wet finger on a wine glass: the ring of light around Maya brightens and hums through the turn (a short note for a quarter turn, a long one for the U-Turn), and each stop is one clear strike. *Twist:* Lumen's glass chime gets a visible source: the robot is playing its own ring.
37. **The groove.** The four cards land on a steady tempo, and idle robots like Theo bob a hair to it, their eyes pulsing faintly on each downbeat. *Twist:* the register becomes a bar of music the whole board feels, and the bobbing says "not your turn yet" without a word.
38. **Moonwalk.** On Move Back, the trail under Maya scrolls forward like a treadmill while she glides backward, her ring pulsing in little forward steps. *Twist:* the moonwalk illusion made of light alone; her body and pointer stay steady, so the rule (backward without turning) is still clear.
39. **Cursive.** The program plays as joined-up handwriting: Maya flows out of each move into the next turn without a dead stop, yet lands on every space and heading exactly on its beat. *Twist:* block capitals become cursive; the trail left on the glass looks like a signature, and the beat timing doesn't change.
40. **Full stop.** After her last card Maya gives a tiny nod, and her ring of light pinches into a bright point for an instant, like the full stop at the end of a sentence. *Twist:* the program is a sentence and the robot punctuates it; the room knows her turn is over without any HUD.
41. **Head tilt.** When a program contradicts itself (forward, then back), the camera gives a puzzled two-degree head-tilt during the Move Back and straightens. *Twist:* the TV is a spectator too, mirroring the room's own "what is she doing?"; it only fires on contradictions, so it stays funny.
42. **Rewind.** Move Back plays like a tape running backward: a faint trail appears first behind her and she swallows it as she goes, her speed curve reversed so she eases out and snaps in. *Twist:* reverse driving shown as reversed time, which feels oddly right and makes Move Back unmistakable from every other move.
43. **Relativity (rare).** On a Rotate Right, the camera orbits a quarter turn around Maya, so she seems to stand still while the whole slab turns around her; then the camera eases home and she is simply facing south. *Twist:* "did she turn, or did the world?"; a once-a-game joke about relative motion that resolves to exactly the rule's result.
44. **Stop-motion (rare).** Once a game, one card plays at eight frames a second with the tiny jitter of a hand-animated model, and the next card is smooth again. *Twist:* it breaks the look for exactly one beat to admit these are all just pieces being moved, and since the glass and light are untouched it comes straight home.
45. **Backing away slowly.** The program becomes a scene: Rotate Right turns Maya to face the room, her eye widens as if she's just noticed everyone watching, she backs away one space without breaking eye contact, then U-Turns as if nothing happened. *Twist:* the players' random program reads as a tiny awkward social moment; in general, any robot that ends a turn facing the camera holds eye contact with the room for a beat.
46. **The living room (rare).** Once a game, when a robot faces the camera, its polished paint reflects a faint warm rectangle and the dark silhouettes of heads on a sofa: the room, watching the TV. *Twist:* the TV becomes a window both ways and the room sees itself in the robot; a few frames of it, gone by the next card.
47. **Nature documentary (rare).** Occasionally a single line of thin type fades in under the robot like a wildlife film's caption: "The Pebble backs up to reconsider." *Twist:* deadpan narration of the most ordinary moment in the game; it only appears on everyday cards, so the joke is the mundanity.
48. **Signature trail.** Up close, the trail isn't a line: it's the player's name written over and over in tiny thin type, MAYAMAYAMAYA, which blurs into a stripe of light from across the room. *Twist:* invisible from the sofa, discovered the first time someone walks up to the TV, and then everyone checks theirs.
49. **Worn glass.** Each turn in place polishes its spot a little: spaces where robots turn often grow a faint bright scuff that builds through the round and clears when a new round starts. *Twist:* the board quietly becomes a heatmap of the round's traffic, like a worn stair, and the busiest junctions glow.
50. **Dolphin (rare).** Once in a long while, Move 2 is a dolphin's arc: Maya dips into the smoked glass, glides the two spaces as a blurred red glow under the surface, and breaches back onto her space facing east. *Twist:* the slab turns out to have an inside; the rule is obeyed one layer down, and she's back on top before the Rotate Right.
