# Checkpoint: the room's verdict

Judged as six friends on a couch watching the TV between turns. Reaching a flag happens a handful of times a game (10 to 15 claims across a table, 20 or more over a night), so each entry was judged on the first time and the twentieth. Scores come from the rendered videos (contact sheets plus frames pulled at 10 to 15 fps), not from the descriptions. The baseline is today's game: two big rings, one red and one white, expanding from the claimer.

Scores are 1 to 10. "Repeat" means it still holds up the twentieth time tonight. "Overall" is a judgement, not an average.

## Winner: 4c-freeze-frame-detour

This is the one the couch laughs at out loud: an impatient robot bolts off the grid, freezes mid-strain at the end of a white-hot leash, then gets twanged home. It also carries the most game information of any entry. Flag 2 lights up in her colour (the race's next goal), her own trail becomes the leash, and her ring drops down flag 1's column, so anyone who looked up late still knows who claimed, where she is and where she's headed. It's built entirely from Lumen's own pieces: the trail, light, and a dimmed room.

**When porting:** Keep the freeze short (about 0.35 s), and drop the punch-in if it starts to drag by mid-game; 4a's hold at full stretch without a freeze is the ready fallback. Aim the bolt at the claimer's real next flag, but cap it at 1 to 1.5 spaces and steer it away from walls, pits and other robots so she never appears to overlap them or fall in. Fade the next-flag tint fully back to white so it can't read as a claim on flag 2. Bring back Theo's name label and the played card exactly as they were. Skip the bolt on the last flag (the win moment takes over), and play the claims one after another when several robots claim in the same register.

## Runner-up: 1c-long-exposure-disk

This is the room's 'ooh': a hoop of her own light spins down like a settling coin, smeared into a glowing bowl by the long exposure, and the speeding wobble is a drumroll everyone can hear coming to an end. It's the purest Lumen image in the set, it ends exactly on the game's taken ring, and the pleasure of watching a coin settle doesn't wear out the way a gag can.

**When porting:** The camera dive, the red flood over the room and the roll on the way back are too much for every claim. Keep the long-exposure smear but use a smaller push-in with no roll, or play it from the wide shot like 1a, so the board isn't gone for 1.5 s. Delay setTaken until the click and swap in the real ring on the same frame. On a flag others already hold, settle on top of their rings so the stack reads as a tally. The whirr-to-click sound carries half the effect, so it has to ship with the sound.
