# Belt: the room's verdict

Judged as six friends on a couch watching the TV between turns. Belts run every register on most courses, so
this is the moment the room sees most often. The baseline slides Maya two spaces and turns her in about 1.2 s.
Every entry below was judged from its contact sheet and from frames pulled from the video.

Scores run from 1 to 10. Overall is a judgement, not an average.

## Winner: 2a-shape-sorter

**Why.** It is the only entry that makes the game's most frequent moment clearer than it is today: before Maya
moves, the room sees where she will stop and which way she will face, in a shape made of the board's darkness and
her own light. The jam, wiggle and drop get a laugh the first few times, and the fit is satisfying enough to keep
watching all night.

**Port.**
- Make the clean drop the default and the jam and wiggle an occasional variant, so it is not a joke on every register.
- Start the slot dark: its first frames are a flat grey tile.
- Keep the coloured rim on the whole time, so the slot never reads as a pit.
- Open a slot for every robot riding at once, and only after the move has been resolved, so a blocked or pushed ride
  never shows a false slot.
- Soften the red bloom at the fill so it doesn't flash every register.
- Give each form its own slot shape, and keep the whole thing within 2.5 s.

## Runner-up: 5c-the-koi

**Why.** It gets the biggest gasp in the set: a fish drawn in the belt's own light, deep under the chevrons,
carries Maya and swings her round, and its long-exposure arc makes the turn clearer than it is today. Because it is
rare, the room starts watching every belt ride for it, which makes the most common moment worth watching.

**Port.**
- Keep it rare: about one ride in fifteen, never two registers in a row, and one robot at a time.
- Right now it swims in from past the belt's first tile, over bare floor. Start it upstream on the belt, or have it
  rise from the depth.
- The light dip and camera lean are fine that rarely, but they must not hide other robots moving in the same register.
- The slow-motion hold still has to fit the 2.5 s budget.
- Make sure its exit down the south leg doesn't suggest that Maya is going that way.

The two go together: the slot on every ride, and now and then the koi delivers her into it. The trapped-in-glass
mirror (3b) and the deadpan rewind (4b) would also work as rare variants, but never as the everyday ride.
