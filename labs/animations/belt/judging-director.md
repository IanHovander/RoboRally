key beat, and a per-frame brightness and difference check to catch flashes and snaps. For comparison, the game does
this moment in **1.13 s** today. Every entry fits the 2.5 s budget (2.20 to 2.47 s), but every entry roughly doubles
the cost of the most frequent moment in the game, so repeat and pacing weighed heavily.

## Winner: 2b-shape-sorter

**Why.** It's the only entry that makes the game state clearer than today. A slot in her own silhouette opens in the
glass and tells the room where she'll stop and which way she'll face before she gets there. Then it pays off with a
quiet, oddly satisfying click and a fill of her own light. It's built only from Lumen's materials (darkness in glass,
a hairline of the player's colour, light that fills), with no camera move, no flash and no jump at the end. That lets
it play on every belt ride without wearing out the room.

**Porting: what to fix or watch.**
- **Time.** It runs 2.47 s against 1.13 s today, on the most frequent moment. Ship an everyday cut of about 1.5 s:
  the slot opens with the first belt beat, then the two steps, a single nudge, the click and the fill. Drop the
  glance and the double-take, and let the fill overlap the next beat. Keep the full version for a robot's first ride.
- **Lookahead.** Beats reach `scene.js` one at a time (`host.js` calls `arena.playBeat` per beat). On the express
  beat, the scene can't know where the all-belts beat will leave her. Either have the engine put the final belt
  position and heading on the express beat, or open the slot on the second beat only.
- **Never read as a pit.** A dark hole is Lumen's pit language, and the Cube's slot is square like a pit. The rim
  and the notch must be lit from the first frame.
- **One slot per moving robot.** Show none for a robot whose belt move is blocked, and none for one carried off the
  board or into a pit (those are falls).
- **Build the distance field once per form** at load.
- **The sink.** Make sure her 0.065 sink into the glass doesn't clip her ring, glow pool or trail.
- **Cut Jun's look-down.** It's invisible at game size and needs logic for bystander robots.

## Runner-up: 5b-the-koi

**Why.** It's the surprise of the set and the most Lumen. Something living in the board's light, soft and drawn
inside the belt, with the chevrons parting over it, carries her two spaces, flicks her round the bend and slips
away. As a rare variant on top of the everyday shape sorter, it makes every belt ride a possible "did you see
that?!", and it renders with no flash, no camera move and no snap.

**Porting: what to fix or watch.**
- **Keep it rare.** About one ride in fifteen, never twice in a row, and never in a busy multi-robot belt beat.
- **Stay inside the belt.** Its path must run only along belt cells. Here it starts west of the belt over bare
  floor, and its tail wisps spill past the belt's edges at the bend.
- **Make the fish read at game size.** Give it a clearer head and body mass, and slow the exit slightly so the two
  eye-lights register.
- **Lookahead.** It merges the express and all-belts beats into one glide, so it needs both beats up front (the
  same lookahead as the winner).
- **The belt shader swap.** The parting chevrons follow a 12-point spine, which has to follow any belt path, curves
  and both corner directions included. The swap back to the stock belt material must be invisible.
- **Time.** It takes about 2.4 s and can't share a ride with the shape sorter. Pick one per ride.
