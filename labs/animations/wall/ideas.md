# Bumping into a wall: 50 Lumen animation ideas

Maya (red Pebble) plays Move 3, glides two spaces east and meets the fin on the east side of (3,1); the third space never happens. Every idea below plays inside the 2.5 s budget (the two-space glide takes about 0.6 s, which leaves roughly 1.5 s for the gag) and ends the way the game needs it: Maya resting on (3,1) facing east, Ava untouched on (5,3) facing west, the wall in place, the camera home, nothing left on the board that a player could misread.

## Banned (too obvious)

- Sparks spraying off the wall at the hit.
- A comic "BONK!" / "CLANG!" word burst.
- Camera shake on impact.
- Dizzy stars or tweeting birds circling the robot.
- A generic white flash or glow burst at the point of contact.
- The robot squashing flat against the wall like a pancake.
- A rubbery boing: bouncing back off the wall and rolling back.
- The wall wobbling like jelly.
- A generic spin-out after the hit.
- A red X, a stop sign or a "BLOCKED" stamp.
- Cracks spreading across the glass, or the wall shattering.
- An instant slow-motion replay of the crash.

## Ideas

1. **Knock, knock.** Maya stops nose to the fin, pauses, then taps it twice with her nose: two tiny chimes, two dots of light blooming on the glass. A beat later one dot answers from the far side of the fin, and she settles. *Twist:* she treats the wall as a door and something on the other side knocks back. The board never says who.
2. **Running in place.** The wall stops her body but not her will: for the third count she keeps pushing in tiny jittery shoves against the fin, and her light trail, which normally measures distance, scribbles itself into a bright red knot on the one spot in front of the wall, then fades. *Twist:* the trail draws the effort instead of the distance, so the lost space turns into a tangle of light.
3. **Rear-ended by her own trail.** She stops dead. A heartbeat later the light trail flowing behind her keeps coming, catches up and taps her from behind, nudging her into the fin a second time (tink... tink), then thins out and fades. *Twist:* Lumen's signature trail has momentum of its own and delivers the second half of a two-part pratfall.
4. **A hair's breadth.** For the last half space she brakes: two thin parallel skid lines of red light streak across the glass and she slides up to the fin. A small round lens of glass glides in over the gap and magnifies it: a hair of light still shows between her nose and the wall. The lens moves on, she creeps that last hair, and: tink. *Twist:* she almost made it. The proof is a piece of Lumen's own glass, and the punchline is the smallest sound in the game.
5. **I meant to do that.** She hits, freezes for one beat, then casually leans her shoulder against the fin, rolling a few degrees onto it like someone leaning on a bar, her eye glancing idly away, before she straightens up facing east. *Twist:* the oldest bit in physical comedy, played by a polished stone that is pretending this was the plan all along.
6. **Tape measure.** A thin ribbon of light unspools ahead of her like a tape measure, marking 1, 2 as she rolls. On 3 its tip slams into the fin, buckles, and zips back into her nose with a quick snap that rocks her slightly. *Twist:* the measuring takes the hit instead of the robot, and everyone knows the sting of a tape snapping back.
7. **Blush.** On impact a deeper, brighter red rises through her polished body from the base up, like blood rising to a face, then drains away as she settles. *Twist:* the player's colour, which only ever means "who", means "how she feels" for one second.
8. **Ava saw that.** On the bump, Ava's capsule across the board slowly turns her eye toward Maya, holds it there, blinks once, slowly, and turns back to face west as if nothing happened. *Twist:* the bystander is the comedy: a capsule's silent judgment is funnier than any effect, and it turns the other robots into an audience.
9. **The impression.** Right after Maya's bump, Ava does a tiny mocking imitation in place: she lurches her nose forward at empty air, rocks back with her own little bonk, and settles facing west. *Twist:* the robots tease each other the way the players on the sofa do, and it costs nothing on the board.
10. **To camera.** After stopping, Maya swivels slowly to look straight out of the TV at the room, holds a deadpan beat, and turns back to face the wall. *Twist:* the robot looks at the human who programmed it, and everyone in the room knows who miscounted.
11. **Lamp, jiggled.** On the bump her eye flickers like a bulb knocked in its socket: off, half on, off. She gives a tiny shiver and it pops back on, steady. *Twist:* her eye is a light, so she reacts the way a lamp does rather than a face, and everyone has jiggled a lamp like that.
12. **Halo.** Instead of dizzy stars, her ring of light peels off the glass, wobbles up and orbits her head once like a loose halo, then drops back to the glass and wraps around her base again. *Twist:* the dizzy-stars cliché is played by a part of the robot itself, so it's made of Lumen and lands back exactly where it belongs.
13. **Question pointer.** Her little facing pointer keeps going when she stops: it crumples against the fin, curls into a question mark of light for a beat, then straightens back into an arrow pointing east. *Twist:* the one part whose job is to say "this way" is the part that gets confused, and it goes back to doing its job, so the facing still reads right at the end.
14. **Six forms, six bumps.** Each form meets a wall its own way. The Orb rolls up, rocks back and forth and comes to rest. The Prism tips onto an edge and drops back. The Capsule wobbles like a weeble. The Cube stops square and dead with one hard tick. The Spire sways like a metronome. The Pebble skims (idea 15). *Twist:* one rule, six personalities, so the most common moment in the game never looks the same twice in a row.
15. **Skipping stone.** Being a Pebble, Maya skims the glass like a stone on a lake: skip, skip, each touch leaving a small ring of light and a clear chime. The third skip comes a little early and ends flat against the fin, sending a ripple of light running up the wall's length. *Twist:* her form's name taken literally. The skips count the move out loud, so the missing third skip is obvious to the ear.
16. **Mirror, mirror.** As she approaches, the fin behaves like a mirror: a reflected Maya glides toward her from the far side, matching every move, and they meet nose to nose at the glass with a chime. The reflection fades. *Twist:* she didn't hit a wall, she ran into herself, and it is the wall's own material that plays the classic mirror gag.
17. **Aquarium face.** At contact the camera swings a few degrees to see the face of the fin, and through the glass her eye shows up enormous, a giant soft white light pressed against the pane like a kid's face at an aquarium. The camera drifts home and the eye shrinks back to normal. *Twist:* refraction is the joke: from the other side of the glass, Maya's pressed-up eye looks the size of a moon.
18. **Prism.** At the hit, the white light of her eye passes into the fin and comes out the far side split: a thin fan of spectrum lies across the glass for a beat, then folds back into a single white line and goes out. *Twist:* Lumen's one-light rule breaks for one beat, the only rainbow in the whole game, and a mistake is what makes it.
19. **Breath on the glass.** Where her nose meets the fin, a soft fog in her colour blooms on the pane like breath on a cold window, then fades slowly over the next beats while the game carries on. *Twist:* a tiny leftover trace of the embarrassment. Anyone watching closely sees the wall still "remembering" her a few seconds later.
20. **Tuning fork.** The fin rings: its bright top edge visibly shivers in a standing wave as the chime sounds, and faint rings of light spread across the glass from it. When they reach Ava, her ring hums back in green. *Twist:* walls are glass instruments. Each wall's pitch comes from where it sits, so over a game the room learns which wall sings which note.
21. **Sea wall.** She stops, but her trail doesn't: the light flowing behind her surges past her, breaks against the fin like a wave on a sea wall, sprays up over the top edge in a glittering arc and rains down as fading motes on the far side. *Twist:* only the spray reaches the third space. The board stays dry and correct.
22. **The slab is water.** The impact sends a ripple through the smoked glass itself as if it were a still pool: the grid lines and the light under the glass wobble outward in rings, Ava bobs gently as it passes under her, and the slab goes still and solid again. *Twist:* the glass the whole game stands on turns out to be liquid, for one second.
23. **Where she isn't.** On the space beyond the wall, (4,1), a Maya-shaped absence appears: the glass's light dims in her exact silhouette, a small hole of missing light where she meant to be, and then slowly fills back in. *Twist:* in Lumen's own language (a pit is where the light is missing), the lost step becomes a negative: we see her by her absence.
24. **The ring slips under.** Her ring of light, which lives on the glass, doesn't know about walls: it slides on under the fin to the next space, sits there alone for a beat, realises, and scurries back under to wrap around her again. *Twist:* a Peter Pan shadow gag, played by the one part of a Lumen robot that is drawn on the glass rather than standing on it.
25. **Mime.** As she arrives, the fin's light goes out and the wall is invisible. She presses at it here and there, and every touch lights a dot on an unseen pane, until her touches have drawn the wall's outline and it relights. *Twist:* Lumen draws its walls with light, so the mime's invisible wall can really be revealed by touch.
26. **Newton's cradle.** She hits the fin and stops dead, and on the far side a small bead of red light pops out of the glass, rolls exactly one space to (4,1), stops and fades. *Twist:* the momentum gets through even though she doesn't. A physics toy finishes the third step for her, and the board state stays correct.
27. **The way around.** She stops, but her trail keeps drawing: it curls neatly around the end of the fin, through (3,0) and (4,0), and arrives on the space she wanted, (4,1), where it sits as a bright curl and fades. *Twist:* the light knows the route she didn't, which gives the player a quiet hint for next time without a word.
28. **Revolving door.** She pushes the fin and it turns like a revolving door, pivoting on its middle and carrying her around with it in one smooth full turn (about 0.8 s). She glimpses the far side for an instant, and the door brings her right back to her own space, facing the fin again. *Twist:* the wall does let her through, all the way around. Geometry keeps the rule, and the spin-out cliché becomes a door joke.
29. **Halfway into the glass.** She doesn't stop at the fin. She slides partway into it, and the half of her inside the wall shows refracted: shifted, paler, glassier. She stalls there, stuck, and the glass squeezes her back out with a soft pop onto her own space. *Twist:* in Lumen a wall is glass, and it turns out you can get into glass but not out of the far side, so the rule holds at the far face.
30. **The wall steps aside (and thinks better of it).** As she approaches, the fin politely slides north out of her way. She starts the third space with a little lift of joy, and the fin, remembering the rules, slides back and gently nudges her home onto (3,1). *Twist:* the wall tries to be nice and the rulebook overrules it. It's an object with a conscience, and it ends back in place.
31. **Glassblower.** She presses into the fin and it bulges like hot glass on a blowpipe: a thin glowing bubble stretches ahead of her into the next space with her red light swirling inside. It cools, firms back to flat and eases her back onto (3,1). *Twist:* the jelly-wall cliché rebuilt from Lumen's one material: for one beat the glass is molten again.
32. **The player's hand.** A large soft hand drawn in thin lines of light reaches in, pinches Maya like a board-game piece and tries to slide her the third space. The hand bonks the wall too, pauses, and sets her down on (3,1), squaring her to face east. *Twist:* the board game underneath shows through, and even the hand of the player obeys the walls.
33. **Rewind.** She hits. The picture rewinds one space, her trail sucking back into her, and she tries again a little faster, hits again, and accepts it, all inside a second. *Twist:* a VCR rewind made of light: the game double-checks its own rule on screen and gets the same answer.
34. **Lost chevron.** The Move 3 card above her lights one chevron per space. At the wall the third chevron, still lit, comes loose, drifts forward, bonks the fin and drops into the glass like a coin into water, going out. *Twist:* what the wall stops is the card's third arrow, so the room literally watches the step she lost.
35. **Stranded 3.** Thin numerals bloom on the spaces she crosses, 1, then 2. The 3 appears on the far side of the wall where it should be, stranded. It peers over the fin, then wilts down into the glass. *Twist:* even the counting has to obey the wall, so the miscount shows up as a number on the wrong side.
36. **Snuffed.** As she starts, the three-dot path her phone previewed is drawn on the glass ahead of her, the same tiny light-table path the player saw. She eats the dots as she goes, and the third dot, beyond the wall, is snuffed out like a candle with a thin curl of light for smoke. *Twist:* plan and reality in one picture: the player sees exactly which of their dots the wall blew out.
37. **Labelled.** After the bump, one word in thin type appears over the fin's top edge: "wall." It fades. Each bump in a game takes the next caption: "wall.", "still a wall.", "yes, that wall.", "wall (since round 1)." *Twist:* the BLOCKED-stamp cliché turned deadpan, and the joke builds across the game for people who keep watching.
38. **Ping.** The bump sends a pulse of light racing across the glass, and as it passes, every wall on the board flashes its top edge in turn, like sonar returns, then everything settles. *Twist:* the embarrassment becomes a public service: in a full game the whole room gets a one-second reminder of where every wall is.
39. **The missing note.** Each space she enters rings one note of a rising three-note phrase and leaves a small ring of light. At the wall the third note never comes: the third ring tries to bloom beyond the fin, gets squashed flat against it into a line, and the phrase hangs unresolved. *Twist:* the room hears the miscount, and an unfinished melody is more maddening, and funnier, than any crash.
40. **Waiting out the count.** Stopped at the wall, she still has to spend the third count: a single hand of light sweeps once around her ring like a clock, at exactly the pace of a step, while she stands perfectly still. *Twist:* the lost step still costs its time, and watching it tick away is gently funny and shows the rule.
41. **Cheers.** The fin leans to meet her like a glass in a toast. They clink with one clear chime and a tiny bright point where they touch, and the fin straightens. *Twist:* in a world made of glass a collision is a toast, and Lumen's glass-chime sound finally meets the moment it was made for.
42. **Wall's-eye view.** For a beat the camera cuts to the far side of the wall, looking back through the frosted fin: a blurred red light grows larger and larger and stops right on the glass. Cut back to home with her settled. *Twist:* we are the wall, seeing the moment from the side of the thing that wins. (It works best as a rare variant, since the moment is common.)
43. **The last centimetre.** Two spaces at full speed, then for the last few centimetres time slows almost to a stop: the room's light dims and her eye flares. Then time snaps back and the contact is the smallest "tink" in the game. *Twist:* the slow-motion cliché used for anticlimax: all that build-up for nothing.
44. **The camera miscounted too.** The camera glides along with her and keeps going one space past the wall when she stops, holds an awkward beat over the empty space, and sheepishly drifts back home. *Twist:* the camera makes the same mistake as the player, and an embarrassed overshoot replaces the shake.
45. **The TV is a mirror.** On the bump, the whole picture fades almost to black for half a second, long enough for the real TV glass to reflect the room: the players on the sofa. Then the board fades back up. *Twist:* the effect is real. Lumen's dark room plus a dark screen turns the actual TV into a mirror, so whoever miscounted sees themself.
46. **Flashbulbs.** At the hit, a few tiny white flashes pop in the darkness around and below the slab, like an audience's phones snapping the moment, then the room goes dark again. *Twist:* the dark room had an audience all along, and Maya's worst moment is the one that got photographed.
47. **Lost and found.** The lost third step, a small chevron of light, slides off the edge of the slab, falls through the dark and lands softly on the floor far below, where it stays glowing faintly. Every lost step of the night joins it. *Twist:* by the end of the game, a little pile of lost steps glows in the dark under the board, a record nobody planned, and the board itself is unchanged.
48. **Down the river.** The third step, a small chevron of light, sinks through the top of the glass, and a faint current under the glass carries it away like a leaf on a river until it slips out of sight at the slab's edge. *Twist:* Lumen's streams under the glass become the board's way of carrying off what was lost: gentle, a little sad, a little funny.
49. **Notches.** Each time a robot bumps a wall, a tiny notch of light in that robot's colour appears on the fin's top edge and stays for the rest of the game. *Twist:* the walls keep score. By the end of the night the wall everyone hit wears a little rainbow of notches, and the room has a favourite wall.
50. **Repeat offender.** The wall reacts according to its history. The first bump gets a soft tink. On the second its top edge brightens with visible exasperation. By the third it slowly turns toward the culprit's colour before fading back. *Twist:* escalation across a whole game turns the most common moment into a running gag that pays off late, and the first bump stays the quietest.

## The five to animate

1. **28. Revolving door.** The biggest "wait, what? again!": the fin carries her all the way round and back. It's one big motion that reads at any size, and she goes round once per lost step, so it varies and quietly teaches the rule.
2. **10. To camera.** The likeliest real laugh, because the deadpan stare lands on the person in the room who miscounted. It's pure motion and one bright eye, and it gets funnier as a running bit.
3. **3. Rear-ended by her own trail.** Second-hit slapstick made from the most visible thing on the board: the long bright trail whips up and bumps her into the fin again. It's short enough to play every time.
4. **18. Prism.** The gasp: the only rainbow in the game, fanned across the glass by a mistake. It's beauty rather than a gag, so it holds up to repetition, and it's made of Lumen's own refraction.
5. **47. Lost and found.** The one that gets better with repetition: the card's third chevron bonks the fin and falls to the floor below, and the night's lost steps pile up there in their players' colours.
