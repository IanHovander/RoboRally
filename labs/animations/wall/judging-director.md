# Wall bump: the director's verdict

Judged on screen (the contact sheets, 10 fps strips of every video and frame-by-frame passes over every impact, camera move and fold) for shipping into Lumen's `scene.js`. The moment is common, so it has to read on a 10 to 13 space board above a 40% HUD and hold up over a whole night. Scores run from 1 to 10. Every entry stays inside the 2.5 s budget, but each adds about 1.4 to 1.6 s over today's 0.87 s bump.

## Winner: 1a-revolving-door

It is the real surprise of the set: the wall lets her through, all the way round, and a full turn keeps the rule. It is also the best-timed piece, with an anticipation hold as the fin gives, weight in the turn, a hitch on the far side, a hard lock with skid and settle, and no camera, flash or room change. One big motion two spaces wide reads at TV size, and the ring of trail left round the fin is a calm Lumen mark of 'she tried'.

**Porting:** Walls are separate groups in arena.board but nothing indexes them, so keep a map from wall edge to group when building them in scene.js; the bright line baked into the glass stays put as the door frame. Her body orbits 0.5 spaces out, so it pokes into the rows either side and sits on the far space at half-turn. Fall back to a smaller wobble when the far space holds a robot, a neighbour is beside the fin, or the wall is on the board edge (common on real courses), or she clips through the robot or swings over the void. Cap the turns per lost step (Move 3 straight into a wall would be three turns, past the 2.5 s budget): make later turns quicker, or two at most. The ring needs sparser trail points (the shared trail holds 90), so check it fades on the normal clock and is never mistaken for the next register's path. Lift the fin's emissive while it turns so its broadside face stays light rather than a grey plank, and check a north/south wall, where the door turns in depth.

## Runner-up: 4c-prism

The gasp: a soft fan of spectrum over the spaces she never reached, the only rainbow of the night, made by a mistake and made of refraction, Lumen's own material. It is the most legible picture of 'blocked here' at any distance, and a thing of beauty holds up on repeat better than a gag does. 4c is the better-built prism, with richer colour than 4a, smooth eased camera moves and a gentle dim.

**Porting:** Every miscount is a lot of times for a room dim and a camera rise: keep the dim shallow and short, and drop the camera move after the first time per game or per player. 4a's static staging with 4c's fan colours is a fine default. Smooth the fold: the fan pops from a wide white wedge to a thin line in one frame (2.50 to 2.53 s), so ease its angle to zero over four or five frames. On a 10 to 13 wide board, clip the fan to the slab and size its reach to the lost steps, so it also says how much move was lost. setDim and the eye light are shared state, so restore both exactly on release.
