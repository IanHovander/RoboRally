# Push: the animation director's verdict

I judged as the person who has to ship one of these into Lumen's `scene.js`. This push happens several times a game,
on a board 10-13 spaces wide with the bottom 40% of the screen given to the HUD. The budget is 2.5 s, and today's
version takes 0.87 s. I looked at every contact sheet, then pulled 10-15 fps strips, close crops and single frames
from each video. I also measured frame-to-frame change and brightness to find flashes and snaps.

Scores run from 1 to 10. Overall is my judgement, not an average.

For the 5x camera dives, repeat is scored as designed, as roughly one push in ten.

## Winner: 3a-tape-measure (Refraction Kiss: the tape measure)

**Why.** It turns the push into one big, simple image made of Lumen's own material. The pusher's warm light goes into
the glass and comes out as a rainbow lying on the glass, which ends where the victim will land, then folds back into
him. It reads in colour and shape from across a room and teaches the rule (one space per space) before the move
happens. It has a real anticipation, a hit-stop, a slosh and a reel-in, and it does all this in 2.1 s without touching
the camera or the HUD.

**Porting.**
- **Direction.** It only runs on puppet poses, the model's `paint`/`eyeMat`, a vertex-coloured plane and two
  sprites, so it ports cleanly. But `X()`, the row and the fan angle are hard-coded east; drive them from the move
  beat's path and direction.
- **Other forms.** Work out the non-Prism versions (the meta's Orb dot, Cube mirror, Capsule caustic) before
  release, since 5 pushes in 6 won't be a Prism.
- **Player colour.** Keep a blue rim on the pushed robot while it fills with white, so the player's colour never
  leaves the board.
- **Chains, walls and repeats.**
  - For a chain push, run the light through each robot.
  - For a wall-blocked push, stop the fan at the wall fin.
  - When several pushes land in one register, use a short version (around 1.2 s) that skips the hold and the hiccup.
- **Clean up.** Restore `paint.emissive` exactly when it finishes.

## Runner-up: 4b-no-dodging (No Dodging (b): on a leash)

**Why.** It gets the biggest laugh, and it reads by silhouette, not detail: a full-space hop away from the lane, then a
T of warm light (the rail to a stop on space 4 and the leash) that snaps him back. It states the rule in one look and
becomes a running gag, it works for any form, and it never moves the camera.

**Porting.**
- **Facing.** Turn only `model.body` for Theo's glances and double-take. Right now the whole robot rotates, so his
  facing pointer says east just before the shove, which contradicts the rule that pushed robots keep their facing.
- **Hop direction.** Pick it from free space (walls, board edge, other robots, pits) and vary it per push.
- **Length.** Cut the sheepish look and tut-tut beat, which can't be seen at 12 spaces wide, to bring it nearer 1.8 s.
- **Rail.** Draw it along the beat's real path, so chain pushes share it and a wall-blocked push ends on the wall.
- **Polish.** Soften or drop the stock landing shockwave ring and the reddish smear on Maya as she revs.
