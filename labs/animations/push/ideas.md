# Push: 50 animation ideas (Lumen)

The moment: Maya (red Pebble) plays Move 2 east. Her first space is free; on the second she shoves Theo (blue Prism, facing the room) one space east. Ends with Maya on 3,2 facing east, Theo on 4,2 still facing south, and Jun (yellow Cube, far corner) untouched. Budget 2.5 s.

## Banned (too obvious)

- Sparks or particles spraying from the point of contact
- A comic POW / BONK / WHAM word
- Camera shake on impact
- A white flash or glow burst at contact
- An expanding shockwave ring on the glass
- Theo spinning like a top or tumbling over
- Bullet-time slow motion at the moment of contact
- Cartoon squash-and-stretch on impact
- Speed lines or motion-blur streaks behind them
- Dazed stars, a "!" or an angry face over Theo
- A wind-up and charge (lean back, rev, launch)
- Billiard-ball transfer: Maya stops dead and Theo shoots off

## Fifty ideas

1. **The Door That Gives.** Maya leans her whole nose into Theo, but he slides away a hair faster than she pushes, so she pitches forward into empty air and catches herself on the glass with a wobble, right on her space and facing east. *Twist:* the joke is on the pusher, not the victim: everyone knows the feeling of shoving a door that suddenly opens.
2. **Sneak Attack.** Theo faces the room, so he can't see Maya coming: she covers her free space in exaggerated tiptoe hops with her eye light dimmed to a sliver, pauses right behind his flank, then shoves in one quick burst. *Twist:* Theo's facing (which the rule keeps fixed) becomes a blind spot, and the room gets to watch the sneak happen before he does.
3. **Jump-Start.** Theo's ridge light breathes slowly and dimly, clearly napping; Maya's shove rolls him like a stalled car, and halfway across the space his light coughs, sputters twice and catches, blazing awake just as he stops. *Twist:* the push looks like a favour: she bump-started him, and his eye flicks left and right as if he has no idea how he got there.
4. **Hip Check.** In the last few centimetres Maya swivels and bumps Theo with her flank like an ice-hockey hip check, then swivels back to face east as they both settle. *Twist:* the Pebble has attitude; on paper it is still a plain Move 2, she just made it personal, and she ends facing exactly the way the rules say.
5. **Static Cling.** As Theo slides free, a thin filament of light stays stretched between Maya's nose and his side like cling film; she waggles to break it and it snaps back into each of them as a tiny bead. *Twist:* the banned sparks, turned into stickiness: the contact leaves something you can watch them awkwardly get rid of.
6. **The Loose Marble.** The jolt knocks Theo's eye light out of him as a little blue bead that bounces along the glass ahead; he slides after it, and it rolls back into his face exactly as he stops. *Twist:* in Lumen an eye is just a light, so of course it can come loose, and the bead lands first, showing where he'll end up.
7. **Hula Hoop.** Theo is shoved out from under his own ring of light, which stays behind on space 3; Maya drives into it, it's the wrong size and colour, so it slips off her and rolls after Theo like a hoop, dropping around him with a wobble. *Twist:* the ring of light, usually just a bit of UI, gets a life of its own and is the most confused party at the scene.
8. **Chalk Outline.** Theo leaves a thin white outline of his Prism silhouette on the glass where he stood, crime-scene style, and Maya parks neatly inside it while it fades under her ring. *Twist:* a dark joke drawn in light; she takes his place literally, and the outline is the victim's form, so it's a different shape for every robot.
9. **Name Tag First.** The jolt knocks the THEO name tag off its perch: it skitters ahead and Theo slides after it, and the tag lands back over his head a beat late and crooked before it straightens. *Twist:* the HUD turns out to be physical, and a little label scrambling to catch up reads instantly from across the room.
10. **Deadpan.** Theo is shoved sideways without flinching or turning; during the slide his eye light slides to the edge of his face nearest Maya in a sideways glare, then, once he has stopped, it slowly returns to centre and holds a long flat look straight at the sofa. *Twist:* "pushed robots keep facing the way they faced" becomes a character trait: he won't dignify her with a turn and gives the room a sitcom look-to-camera instead.
11. **After You.** Maya slows to a stop with her nose a hair from Theo and blinks her eye twice ("excuse me"); Theo dips his ridge light in a bow and glides one space aside like a doorman, and Maya rolls in. *Twist:* the room braces for a crash and gets manners, but it is still the same push and ends in the same spaces.
12. **Free Ride.** On contact Theo leans back and rides the shove like a skateboarder grabbing a tow, gliding with a little lean, and hops off neatly at his space. *Twist:* the victim is delighted: a free space east, and maybe he was heading that way anyway.
13. **The Spectator.** Jun, alone in the far corner, turns his visor toward the noise, winces as Theo slides (sinks a few millimetres, visor light narrowing), then slowly turns back west as if he saw nothing. *Twist:* the reaction shot belongs to the robot who wasn't involved, the audience's stand-in on the board.
14. **Paparazzo.** At contact Jun's visor fires like a camera flash from across the board, and a tiny print of that exact frame develops in Jun's corner of the screen, going from dark to image to nothing. *Twist:* the banned flash, moved to a bystander with a motive; the photo is the real frame, so every push gets its own snapshot.
15. **Conservation of Momentum.** A quarter-second after Theo stops, Jun, three spaces away and touching no one, gets jolted a hair west and back as if hit, and glances around indignantly. *Twist:* a phantom Newton's cradle: the push travels through thin air to the one robot who had nothing to do with it, and he ends exactly where he was.
16. **Refraction Kiss.** As Maya's nose touches Theo, her eye light passes through his glass Prism and splits into a small rainbow fan on the glass, landing exactly on space 4; he slides along his own rainbow into it, and the spectrum folds back into his ridge. *Twist:* a prism doing the one thing prisms do, and the rainbow quietly shows where he's going (an Orb could lens it instead, a Cube could mirror it).
17. **Whose Move Was It.** Maya's red trail doesn't stop at her nose: it flows through Theo, and he leaves a red trail for his one space instead of a blue one, blushing back to blue as it fades. *Twist:* Lumen's trails are history, and this one records cause, so anyone glancing up late can see that Theo moved on Maya's card.
18. **The Reflection Is Late.** Theo's faint reflection in the smoked glass stays on space 3 for a beat while he slides away, then scurries after him and slips back under him. *Twist:* the material does the double take; a physical-comedy beat made of nothing but reflection.
19. **Shadow Play.** The board's light spilling on the dark floor far below throws huge soft shadows of the two robots, and the shadows overact: shadow-Maya heaves an enormous shove and shadow-Theo is flung across the floor, while up on the glass real Theo slides one sober space. *Twist:* darkness as a material: the melodrama plays out where the light isn't, and the board game above stays perfectly polite.
20. **Chladni.** The contact makes the slab ring like a struck plate: a fine geometric standing-wave pattern of warm light shivers across the whole glass, nodal lines and all, and fades in from the edges. *Twist:* the banned shockwave turned into real physics: the board is an instrument, and each pair of robots strikes a different note and draws a different figure.
21. **Colour Bleed.** Where Maya's nose touches, her red seeps into Theo's blue glass like ink in water, blooming violet through him as he slides, then draining out of his base into the glass and fading. *Twist:* for one beat two players' colours mix, so the bruise is literally a blend of who did it to whom.
22. **Slosh.** Theo is a glass full of light: the shove makes his inner glow slosh to his trailing side and climb the wall, then slop back past centre and settle in two smaller waves after he stops. *Twist:* the robot is a vessel, and inertia is shown in light instead of motion lines.
23. **Bumped Lamp.** The room's one warm light swings like a pendant lamp someone knocked into: every highlight and reflection on the robots and the glass sways back and forth and settles. *Twist:* the camera-shake cliché moved into the light itself, so the dark room suddenly feels like a real room that someone just bumped.
24. **Clink.** Contact rings both bodies like two wine glasses touching: a bright highlight races around each robot's rim and a fine, fast shiver runs through both, dying away as Theo slides. *Twist:* the push is a toast; glass meeting glass, the one sound Lumen already makes, shown as the thing that makes it.
25. **Long Exposure.** The two trails linger slightly longer and freeze into one light-painting on the glass: a red stroke meeting a blue stroke, with a bright kink where they touched, like a photograph of the event, before it fades. *Twist:* the trail becomes a signature: every push draws its own little glyph, and players start to recognise "the shove shape".
26. **Lights Out.** At the instant of contact the board's light goes out and the room is black except for the eyes; two eyes jostle in the dark while red and blue trails scribble a brief scuffle, then the light comes back up with everyone in place and Theo's eye wide. *Twist:* the cartoon "fight in the dark" in Lumen's own language, and because trails are light, you can still read the move in the dark.
27. **Undertow.** From Maya's nose a short stream of red light runs under the glass to space 4, exactly like a belt, and Theo is carried along it; the stream drains away behind him. *Twist:* Lumen's word for "moved by something else" is a stream under the glass, so for one second Maya becomes a conveyor belt, in her own colour so nobody mistakes it for a real one.
28. **The Second Chevron.** Maya's Move 2 card hovers above her with its two chevrons; the first is spent on her free step, and the second peels off the card, flies onto Theo and carries him the space as she follows. *Twist:* the card explains the rule "one space per space it moves" by itself: the room can see which half of Maya's card Theo got.
29. **The Hand of the Game.** At contact the robots go limp like board-game pieces: Theo is lifted a few centimetres as if by invisible fingers, slid across one space and set down with a soft settle, and Maya is nudged in after him. *Twist:* a reminder that this is a game on a table: for one beat the robots lose their motors and become pieces.
30. **Plan vs Theo.** As Maya's card plays, her programmed path draws itself ahead of her in faint dotted light, like the light table on her phone, and runs straight into Theo; then a blue dotted line extends one space beyond him, the consequence drawn in advance, before it all happens. *Twist:* the phone's preview arrives on the big screen as foreshadowing, so the room sees the doom coming and gets to groan before it lands.
31. **Whack-a-Mole.** On contact Theo is bonked down into the glass like a pressed key, flush with the surface, while his light runs under the glass to the next space, where he pops up with a springy overshoot. *Twist:* arcade logic: he isn't slid, he's punched down and comes up next door, and because the glass stays lit it never reads as a fall.
32. **Snap to Grid.** Theo slides frictionless and overshoots toward space 5, then space 4's thin gridlines brighten like a magnet, tug him back, and he clicks into its centre with a tiny bright notch on the line. *Twist:* the board quietly enforces the rules; it's a joke about pieces snapping to squares, and the click teaches "exactly one space".
33. **Telestrator.** At contact the picture freezes and a thin white sports-analyst's pen scribbles over it: an arrow "2" for Maya, an arrow "1" for Theo, a loop around his pointer marked "still facing you"; then it wipes away and play resumes. *Twist:* Monday Night Football for robots: a rules lesson that is also a joke, drawn in thin hand-drawn light.
34. **Uh-Oh.** The glass under each space lights like a piano key as a robot lands: Maya's two spaces step up, then Theo's space lights a falling minor third lower, the "uh-oh" interval, and all three keys fade together. *Twist:* the board sings what everyone in the room is saying, and since every push plays the same two notes, the room starts singing along.
35. **Gyroscope.** As Theo is shoved his body tries to twist with the impact, but his facing pointer stays nailed to south like a compass needle, and it hauls the body back into line. *Twist:* "pushed robots keep facing" made physical: the pointer is a gyroscope that won't budge, and the room learns the rule by watching it win the fight.
36. **Theo-Cam.** On a rare push (say one in ten), the camera drops to the glass just beside Theo, with Maya's eye charging at the lens like headlights; at contact the camera itself gets shoved one space along with Theo, then floats back up home. *Twist:* the viewer is the one who gets pushed, a camera move that is also a physical gag, kept rare so it stays special.
37. **The Startled Camera Operator.** The camera drifts a little to follow Maya, as cameras do, then is caught out when Theo pops forward ahead of her: a quick, slightly late reframe to catch him, a small overshoot, and an embarrassed settle home. *Twist:* the camera has a personality (the nature-film operator surprised by the animal), and it's small enough to happen on every push.
38. **Relativity.** At contact the camera locks onto Theo, so on screen he never moves: the board and Jun slide one space west under the pair, as if the world shifted and he stood still, and then the camera eases home. *Twist:* from Theo's point of view he was never pushed; a tiny physics joke that resolves the moment the camera lets go.
39. **Local Slow Motion.** Slow motion only inside a soft bubble of glass around the contact: inside it Maya and Theo move like syrup and their trails thicken, while outside Jun keeps bobbing at normal speed, visibly fidgeting; the bubble pops and the two catch up. *Twist:* bullet time with a bystander stuck in real time, and his impatience is the funny part.
40. **Stop-Motion.** For just the push, the two robots drop to eight held poses a second, like a hand-animated tabletop film, while their trails, the light and Jun stay smooth. *Twist:* the way it's animated is the joke: a sudden handmade texture that says "toys", and it costs nothing but holding poses.
41. **Referee's Loupe.** As they meet, a disc of the glass lifts out of the slab and becomes a lens floating over the contact point, magnifying Maya's nose against Theo's facet three times over, then it sinks back flat and is gone. *Twist:* refraction as replay review: the board's own glass checks the call, and on a TV across the room the magnified contact reads from the sofa.
42. **Nature Documentary.** A line of thin type fades in at the bottom like a wildlife subtitle: "The Pebble asserts her territory." Each push gets a different caption keyed to the forms involved ("The Prism, unbothered, relocates."). *Twist:* nature-documentary narration for robots, dry and endlessly varied, and set in Lumen's thin type so it reads like a gallery placard rather than a gag.
43. **Silent Film.** At contact the picture drains to soft black and white with a faint flicker and an intertitle in thin type reads "She did not stop."; colour floods back as Theo comes to rest. *Twist:* slapstick's birthplace, borrowed for one second; Lumen is already mostly warm white light, so the jump to grey and back is small and elegant.
44. **The Dark Gasps.** At the instant of contact the dark room around the slab reveals hundreds of tiny warm points of light, like phones held up in a dark audience; they jolt together in a gasp and fade back to black. *Twist:* the dark room was full of spectators all along, a mirror of the real people on the sofa holding their phones.
45. **Boomerang.** The shove looks so hard that Theo shoots east off the edge of the board and the room gasps, but he arcs out around the dark void in a long glowing loop, swings back over the slab and drops precisely onto space 4, facing south. *Twist:* a fake-out disaster (driving off the board) that lands on the exact legal space; the fall's colour and sound are kept out, so nobody thinks he took damage.
46. **No Dodging.** Just before contact Theo hops up and sideways, trying to step out of the lane, but the lane lights up as a rail of light and yanks him back down into line like a leash, and he gets shoved anyway. *Twist:* the victim tries to cheat and the rules physically refuse; "you can't dodge a push" in a single look.
47. **Grudge.** The game remembers who pushes whom: the first time Maya shoves Theo it's a plain push, the second time his ridge light is already narrowed as she approaches, and the third time he braces and sags before she even arrives. *Twist:* variation as a running gag across the whole game: the room laughs at the callback, not the push.
48. **Signature Shoves.** Each form pushes in its own way: the Pebble nose-flicks like a seal, the Orb rolls in like a bowling ball, the Cube shoulders, the Capsule rams like a battering ram, the Spire leans over and taps with its tip, the Prism slices in edge-first. *Twist:* a very frequent moment stays fresh because it is really six moves, and the room learns each robot's personality from how it shoves.
49. **Signature Landings.** The pushed robot lands in its own way too: a Pebble skips like a stone on water leaving small rings of light, an Orb rolls and rocks, a Cube tips onto an edge and drops flat, a Capsule rolls on its long axis like a pill, a Spire sways like a metronome, a Prism teeters on its ridge. *Twist:* combined with the shoves above there are thirty pairings, so repeat viewing keeps finding new ones.
50. **Fingerprint.** Where Maya's nose touched, a small red smudge of light stays on Theo's flank like a fingerprint on a glass, and fades over the next second and a half. *Twist:* a tiny detail for repeat viewers: evidence in her colour, gone before anyone can argue about it.

## The five to animate

1. **7. Hula Hoop.** A prop with a personality: the ring of light, the biggest and brightest thing a robot has, is left behind, gets worn by the wrong robot, rolls after Theo like a hoop and settles like a spun coin. It's a laugh that reads on a small robot, and it's light enough to enjoy every time.
2. **44. The Dark Gasps.** A reveal about the world: the dark room was full of a phone-lit crowd all along, mirroring the real sofa. It reads as one whole-screen motion, and it holds up like a crowd's "ooh" because its size follows how cruel the push was.
3. **16. Refraction Kiss.** Pure beauty: Lumen's one warm light breaks into a rainbow through Theo's Prism for a beat and quietly marks where he lands. It's a gasp rather than a laugh, it comes straight home to the look, and each form bends the light its own way.
4. **46. No Dodging.** The victim's futile escape: Theo hops out of the lane and a rail of light snaps him back, so the room learns "you can't dodge a push" and laughs harder each time it knows it's coming.
5. **36. Theo-Cam.** The rare "again!": the camera drops to the glass and gets shoved along with Theo, so the viewer is the one pushed. It's huge on any TV, and kept rare so it never wears out while the other four carry the everyday pushes.
