# Driving off the edge: 50 animation ideas (Lumen)

Every idea fits the 3.5 s budget (the Move 3 drive across (5,1) and (6,1) takes about 0.9 s of it) and ends on the
scenario's "after": Maya off the board and out of sight, Ava resting on (2,3) facing north, the camera home, and
nothing of Maya's left on the glass.

## Banned (too obvious)

- Teetering on the rim and windmilling for balance before going over.
- The cartoon hang: hovering in mid-air past the edge, a look to camera, then the drop.
- Tipping and tumbling end over end into the dark while fading out (close to what the game does today).
- A falling-star streak with a descending whistle.
- The tiny, delayed puff or flash far below when she lands.
- A camera shake or thud on impact.
- Sparks, or a burst or ring of glow at the spot on the rim where she went over.
- Slow motion on the fall.
- The camera diving down after her.
- A red screen flash, or "OUT!", "FALL!" or a skull slapped across the screen.
- Skid marks and screeching brakes at the edge.
- The other robot bouncing with laughter or pointing at her.

## The ideas

1. **Squeak down the window.** Maya goes over the rim and, instead of dropping, sticks flat against the slab's glass side face, squashed, and slides slowly down it, smearing a streak of her red light down the glass; at the bottom of the slab she peels off into the dark and the smear fades. *Twist:* the cartoon slide down a window pane, done in Lumen's own glass and light, on the one face of the board nobody ever looks at.
2. **Fetch.** On the last space Maya brakes, but her eye (the light at her front) doesn't: it keeps going on its own momentum, sails off the rim in a little arc and drops; her body watches it go for a beat, then trundles after it and tips over. *Twist:* she leaves the board exactly as the rules say, just not on purpose; she's a dog chasing her own ball of light.
3. **Raft.** As Maya drives toward the east edge, the whole floating slab dips under her weight like a raft, tilting a few degrees toward her; when she rolls off, it springs back, bobs twice and settles level, with Ava rocking on her space like a seasick passenger. *Twist:* "a slab of glass floating in a dark room" taken literally; you only feel her weight once it's gone.
4. **Bungee.** She drops off the edge, and her trail of light, still lying on the glass, stretches down after her like elastic and yanks her back up level with the rim; for one hopeful moment she's almost home, then the trail thins, snaps with a soft pop of light, and she's gone while the loose end whips back across the glass and fades. *Twist:* the trail, which only ever remembers, is suddenly holding her up; hope offered and withdrawn in half a second.
5. **Dismount.** At the rim Maya springs off in one clean gymnast's tuck, lands it perfectly on nothing, and drops; three little lit slides flip up over the dark like judges' scorecards, in thin type: 9.8, 9.9, −2. *Twist:* the third judge is the rulebook, and her 2 damage arrives as a score.
6. **Finish tape.** As she approaches, a ribbon of warm light stretches across the east rim like a finish line; her eye brightens, she speeds up, breaks the tape (its two halves flutter away as light and dissolve) and carries straight on off the edge. *Twist:* for one second she thinks she's won; the edge of the board dressed up as victory.
7. **The card insists.** Maya stops on the last space with her nose over the rim and her eye tipped down at the dark, then edges back a little; over her head the Move 3 card's third chevron starts to pulse, tap, tap, like a foot tapping, until she sags (a small squash) and drives over. *Twist:* the program is a contract she can't get out of; the villain isn't gravity, it's her own card.
8. **Basement league.** She lands softly on the dark floor far below, inside the faint spill of the board's light, and, undeterred, runs the rest of her program down there in miniature (a turn, a step, a smug little U-turn), drawing tiny faint trails on the floor, until her ring winks out. *Twist:* she's out of the round but still committed to the plan; nobody up there is counting, and that's the joke.
9. **Skipping stone.** She's a Pebble, so she skims: off the edge she meets the darkness flat and skips, one, two, three, across it, each touch spreading a ring of light over the black as if it were a still lake, and on the third she sinks with a last small ripple. *Twist:* her form's name taken literally and the void revealed as water; the three skips echo the Move 3 she played.
10. **Ava's torch.** After Maya drops, Ava turns toward the edge and her eye becomes a torch: a soft cone of warm light sweeps over the rim into the dark, finds a tiny red glint far below, and the glint blinks twice back (I'm fine); the torch clicks off and Ava turns back to face north. *Twist:* a robot's eye, which normally just shows which way it faces, becomes a search party, and the dark answers.
11. **Slow clap.** As Maya disappears, Ava's ring of light pulses once, then after a long pause again, then a little faster, each pulse a ripple rolling across the glass toward the empty edge: a slow clap made of light. *Twist:* the "other robot laughs" cliché boiled down to its driest form; the pause before the second clap is where the room laughs.
12. **Every form falls in character.** The exit depends on the robot's form: the Pebble skims (idea 9), the Orb rolls off and bounces once on the rim, the Prism splits the board's light into a brief spectrum as it tips, the Capsule spins away like a dropped pill, the Cube lands flat on the floor below as a square of light, and the Spire dives like a lawn dart and sticks upright before fading. *Twist:* falls happen a few times a game, so the variety comes from who falls; players learn their own robot's signature exit and wait for it.
13. **The ring stays home.** Maya goes over, but her ring of light stops dead at the rim like a dog at the end of its lead; it wobbles, shrinks a little, looks about, then slides over the edge after her like a dropped coaster and goes out. *Twist:* the ring that follows every robot without fail finally gets a personality: loyal, confused and late.
14. **The reflection didn't get the news.** As she drives off, her faint reflection in the smoked glass doesn't follow: the mirrored Maya stays on the last space, turns its pointer toward the edge where she went, and quietly fades into the glass. *Twist:* the board's reflection has a mind of its own; a ghost of the move, one beat behind the rules.
15. **The colour stays.** As she tips over, her red pours out of her like liquid light and runs back up onto the rim, gathering into a bead that sinks into the glass and fades; what falls is a clear, colourless pebble that vanishes the instant it leaves the board's light. *Twist:* the board keeps the player and drops only the body (she will be back), and the fall itself is nearly invisible, which is somehow funnier.
16. **Cheshire exit.** She drops into the dark and goes out in pieces, each on its own beat with a little glass note: the polished body first, then the ring, then the pointer, until only her eye is left, a red dot in the void, which narrows like a closing eyelid and is gone. *Twist:* the fall as a disappearing act in rhythm, with the eye getting the last word.
17. **Strobe.** For the fall only, Lumen's light trail turns into a multi-flash photograph: a chain of ghost Mayas hangs in the dark along her curve off the edge, spaced wider and wider as she speeds up, and then the whole chain fades together. *Twist:* the famous stroboscopic photo of a falling ball, made from the trail Lumen already draws; physics class as a light show.
18. **Light-pipe lap.** When she leaves the glass, the lit rim picks up her red where she went over and carries it around the whole perimeter of the slab like light in an optical fibre, passing behind Ava (whose ring flickers as it goes by), until it meets itself back at the gap and fades. *Twist:* the board does a lap of shame on her behalf; the rim, normally just a bright edge, turns out to be a light pipe.
19. **Ink in water.** She drops past the rim and dissolves into the dark like a drop of ink in water: slow curls of red light bloom and drift beneath the board, thinning until there is only darkness again. *Twist:* the cruellest thing in the game drawn as the calmest, most beautiful thing on screen; the room goes quiet for a second before it laughs.
20. **Meets her reflection.** The dark floor far below turns out to be a mirror: as she falls, a reflected Maya rises from beneath it to meet her, the two touch nose to nose exactly at the floor in a pinpoint of red, and both go out together. *Twist:* Lumen's reflections turned into a tiny romance; the landing isn't an impact, it's a meeting.
21. **Surface tension.** At the rim the edge of the board resists: a curved sheet of faint light bulges out ahead of her like the skin of a soap bubble, stretching with her, until she pops through and the edge snaps back flat with a shiver of light running along it. *Twist:* the edge of the world gets a physical moment of reluctance, and then lets her through anyway.
22. **Orbit.** Off the edge she doesn't drop: she curves down and swings right under the slab, her red glow seen from below through the smoked glass as she passes beneath Ava, rises up beside the west rim (never above the glass), and then the orbit decays and she spirals off into the dark. *Twist:* the board is a planet for one lap, and the glass is lit from underneath by a robot's colour for the only time in the game.
23. **Sky lantern.** Instead of falling, she floats up off the edge, rocking gently, rising into the dark like a paper lantern, until her eye goes out with a tiny blink; unlit, she drops like the stone she is, past the board and into the dark. *Twist:* a robot made of light behaves like a lantern, and gravity only remembers her once the light goes out.
24. **Slurp.** She drops off the edge; a beat later the trail of light still lying on the glass is sucked over the rim after her like a strand of spaghetti, slowly at first, then whipping off the last space with a flick. *Twist:* the trail that normally fades politely is inhaled by the void instead; a tiny gag that makes the dark feel hungry.
25. **Gravity well.** As she reaches the rim, a faint grid of light on the glass near the east edge sags into a funnel, the rubber-sheet picture of gravity from every physics textbook, and she rolls down it and off; then the grid springs back flat with a ripple and fades. *Twist:* the board explains gravity as a diagram drawn in its own lines of light; an inside joke for the science teacher in the room.
26. **Down belt.** Where she goes over, the slab's side face lights up as a stream of light flowing downward, a conveyor belt running down the glass into the dark, and she rides it down calmly, still facing east, until it carries her out of the light and switches off. *Twist:* the fall told in the board's own vocabulary (belts carry robots), as if falling off were just one more board element.
27. **Sat-nav.** As the card plays, her route appears on the glass as three soft arrows of light, like the path preview on her phone: two on the glass, the third drawn on thin air past the edge; she follows them faithfully off the board, and the third arrow tips over after her. *Twist:* the "followed the sat-nav into a lake" joke, with the game's own path preview as the culprit.
28. **Counting out loud.** Thin numerals of light rise from the glass as she enters each space, 1, then 2, and the 3 appears in mid-air beyond the rim, sways, and topples into the dark after her. *Twist:* the card's own number takes the fall; the room counts along and groans on "three".
29. **Program spills.** As she tips over, her remaining program pours out of her: four small lit slides, the cards for her next registers, flutter down through the dark like leaves, turning and catching the light, and wink out one by one. *Twist:* "her program for the round is cancelled" becomes visible, and oddly beautiful; you watch her plan fall away.
30. **The damage follows.** A beat after she's gone, two small shards of glass light (her 2 damage) appear on the rim, peer over the edge, and hop off after her one at a time like dutiful lemmings, going out as they leave the board's light. *Twist:* the penalty literally comes after you; the damage gets tiny personalities, and the second shard hesitates.
31. **Hawk-Eye.** The moment she's gone, the camera snaps to a top-down view for one second: her trail is redrawn as a tennis line-call replay, the mark of her ring landing just past the board's line, and a thin-type "OUT" glows at the rim before the camera goes home. *Twist:* sports-TV grammar for a call that obviously needed no review; the pointless replay is the joke.
32. **Fire exit.** As she nears the edge, a little exit sign lights up above the rim, a running figure in thin lines of light drawn in Maya's red; she drives out under it, the figure in the sign trips out of its own frame and falls, and the sign switches off. *Twist:* building signage confirming this was always a way out; the pictogram's own fall is the punchline.
33. **Swear jar.** As she falls, the board's spill of light finds a faint glass jar on the floor of the dark room; she drops neatly in with a soft clink of light, onto a few pebbles of colour from earlier falls, and the jar sinks back into darkness. *Twist:* the game quietly keeps score of every fall in players' colours, and at the win screen the jar is lit up for everyone to see who fell most.
34. **Missing note.** Each space she crosses lights up under her like a key on a glass xylophone, rising, one, two; the third key isn't there, just a gap of darkness past the rim; then the first two keys play again, slowly, downward, as she disappears. *Twist:* the melody is built from her own path; the edge is a rest in the music, and the two-note reply is the board's sympathy (or its mockery).
35. **Glass glissando.** As she falls, a ladder of faint glass bars appears below the rim, each one lighting and ringing as she drops through it, a run down a xylophone that ends on the lowest, dimmest bar as she goes dark. *Twist:* the fall gets its own scale, and the length of the run tells you how far down the dark goes.
36. **The drop.** The board's light breathes in time with the registers; as she goes over, everything cuts out together for exactly one beat (Ava's ring, the rim, the trails, the room's glow), like the silence before a DJ's drop, and when the beat comes back the board is lit as before and she's gone. *Twist:* the oldest pun in dance music played straight: the drop is literally a drop.
37. **Camera leans over.** As she goes over, the camera tilts and edges forward like someone leaning over a balcony rail, peers down at a tiny red dot in the darkness, holds, and straightens back up as if hoping nobody noticed. *Twist:* the camera becomes a nosy bystander with body language, and how small the dot is, is the joke.
38. **Undo, denied.** She falls; then the picture runs backward (her trail rewinds, she flies back up onto the rim and reverses onto the last space, as if the game were offering an undo), and then it plays forward again exactly the same, a little faster, and she goes over a second time. *Twist:* the rewind teases a mercy the rules don't allow; determinism as comedy, and the second fall is funnier than the first.
39. **Stop-motion.** Everything stays silky except Maya, whose fall suddenly plays at six frames a second, in held poses like a claymation puppet nudged by hand, her trail drawn as separate dots; once she's gone, the world is smooth again. *Twist:* it breaks the look for exactly one second; the stutter reads as panic and is inexplicably funny.
40. **Iris out.** As she tips over, her ring of light leaves the glass, swells to the edges of the screen, then closes in on her like the iris at the end of an old cartoon, darkness outside it, tightening to a circle around her falling eye; then it opens back out on the board without her. *Twist:* the most worn-out cartoon ending, made of her own ring of light.
41. **Helmet cam.** For the fall, a small pane of glass opens in a corner of the picture showing Maya's own view: the rim rushing up, the board tipping away overhead, Ava's ring shrinking to a green dot, then black; the pane dims and closes. *Twist:* you live the fall from inside the robot, while the main picture stays calm and readable.
42. **From underneath.** The camera dips under the slab for a beat and looks up through the smoked glass: Ava's ring glows above like a moon, and Maya's silhouette tips over the bright rim and drops past the lens; then the camera rises home. *Twist:* the only time anyone sees the board from below; the glass lit from underneath is gorgeous, and it makes the drop feel real.
43. **Off the TV.** She drives past the rim and straight off the right side of the TV picture, as if the screen's edge were the board's edge; a beat later she falls in from the top of the screen, in front of the board, and on out the bottom. *Twist:* the TV wraps around like an old arcade game but the board doesn't; the fall happens to the screen, not the slab.
44. **Into the living room.** She tips over the edge and falls toward us instead of down: the red Pebble rushes at the lens until her polished surface fills the screen, the room's softbox lights glinting in it for a frame, then a cut, and the board is calm without her. *Twist:* the fourth wall is the cliff; the room flinches because, for a beat, she's falling at them.
45. **The nameplate.** She falls, but her name label stays hovering over the rim for a beat, realises, and drops too, out of the 3D scene and down the screen, bounces once on the bottom edge of the TV picture, and slips out of the frame. *Twist:* the interface obeys gravity; even the HUD can fall off the board.
46. **House lights.** As she goes over, the dark room's lights come up like a theatre's house lights: a plain grey studio, the slab floating at waist height, and Maya sitting on the floor below looking sheepish, in plain view; then the lights fade down again and she goes with the dark. *Twist:* it breaks the look for one beat; the void was only a room with the lights off.
47. **The board's best effort.** As she reaches the rim, a single pane of light unrolls from the edge under her front like a hastily laid extra tile; she rolls onto it, it holds for a heartbeat, and then she sinks straight through it, because it's only light, and it fades behind her. *Twist:* the board tries to save her with the only thing it's made of, light, which famously can't hold anything.
48. **Question mark.** After she falls, her fading trail of light curls at the rim into a small question mark, its dot the last place her ring touched the glass, then fades as trails do. *Twist:* a tiny detail for the second viewing: the trail is as puzzled as the player.
49. **Tally marks.** Every fall in the game scratches a small notch of light into the slab's side face exactly where the robot went over, in its colour, never on the playing surface; over a game the rim collects a scruffy record of every mistake. *Twist:* the board remembers; regulars start spotting the danger zones before anyone falls.
50. **Diminishing interest.** The first fall of the game gets the full show; each one after gets lazier: the camera stops turning to look, then Ava doesn't bother to turn, then the lights don't dip, and by the fifth a thin-type "again?" glows for a moment over the gap. *Twist:* the game develops opinions, and its mounting boredom is funnier than any explosion.
