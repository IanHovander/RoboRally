## Winner: 2c-off-the-tv

**Why.** This is the one that makes six people shout and point: she drives off the TV, the picture sits empty
with a red glow, and then she falls right past the lens, the biggest thing on screen. The camera never moves, so
the board, Ava and the trail running past the rim stay readable throughout. The fixed "out the side, back in from
the top" logic turns into a running gag the room waits for, not a surprise that is spent after one viewing.

**Port.** Define the wrap for every edge and framing. East and west can leave by the nearest side of the picture,
but north (away from the camera) and south (toward it) need their own route, and a board that fills the TV leaves
little room between rim and screen edge. Trim the gap between leaving and dropping in (about 0.9 s now) toward
the 0.3 to 0.5 s the pick intended, so it does not drag on repeats. The lens pass needs a smooth, high-detail
robot (facets show at that size) and should lean on light streaks rather than photographic leaks. Hold other
robots' beats while she covers the board, and stagger two falls in the same register.

## Runner-up: 1c-bungee

**Why.** The trail, which only ever records where she has been, catches her like elastic, hauls her back to rim
level for a held breath, then snaps with a pop of light and whips home. That gives a gasp and then a laugh, made
entirely of Lumen's own material. It is the best-crafted of the five, and a slightly different rebound each time
keeps it fresh.

**Port.** The push-in and iris take the whole board off screen for about 1.7 s, so the camera wish has to give
way when other robots are moving. Drop or soften the ring that blooms on her starting square after the whip,
because it can read as a reboot marker. Plan a side view for north and south edges, where the drop happens behind
or in front of the slab, and keep the held breath short so the tenth fall plays no slower than the first.
