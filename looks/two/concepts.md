# Look two: fifty directions

Each line is a whole-experience direction: world, camera, light, palette, type, motion, sound, and how the phone feels.

1. **Pop-Up Paper Theatre** (picked). The course is a pop-up book page under a warm desk lamp. Walls, flags, lasers and robots stand up as printed cardboard cutouts. A layered cut-paper factory skyline sits behind. Paper foley and kalimba. The phone is a hand of thick printed cards.
2. **Tin Toy Parade.** A 1950s lithographed tin playset: wind-up robots with turning keys, printed tiles with crimped tabs, clockwork ratchets and a music box.
3. **Risograph Rally.** Three fluorescent inks with misregistration and grain. Every frame looks like a two-pass print, with overprint where robots cross.
4. **Swiss Grid.** International Typographic Style in black, white and one red. Robots are geometric primitives, and only the numbers move like kinetic type.
5. **Sumi-e Circuit.** Brush ink on rice paper. Robots are ink-wash creatures, checkpoints are red hanko seals, and lasers are single brush strokes. Wood blocks and shakuhachi.
6. **Blueprint Night Shift.** A drafting-table blue sheet whose white linework redraws itself as robots move. Pencil scratches; phone cards look like spec slips.
7. **Monument Fog.** Pastel low-poly architecture floating in fog, seen from an isometric camera. Belts are impossible stairs. Soft ambient synths.
8. **Clay Garage.** Plasticine robots with thumbprints animated in 12 fps stop motion, with squash and stretch, on a felt course.
9. **Heirloom Board Game.** A lacquered wood board with brass inlays, carved robots and felt card mats under a pendant lamp. Wooden clacks and card shuffles.
10. **Ben-Day Comic.** Halftone dots, thick ink and CMYK primaries. Panels slam in for big moments, and POW bursts are shapes without words.
11. **Brick Builders.** Glossy studded bricks. Robots snap together and shatter into parts in pits. Click-clack sound.
12. **Synth Grid.** A magenta wireframe grid under a chrome sunset, with neon light-cycle robots and an outrun bassline.
13. **Brutalist Yard.** Raw concrete slabs, sodium lamps, deep shadows and heavy monolithic robots.
14. **16-bit Arcade.** Pixel sprites, CRT curvature, chiptune, and a phone that is literally a gamepad.
15. **Memphis Party.** Squiggles, a terrazzo floor and bouncing geometric robots in pastels and primaries, with 80s jingle stings.
16. **Origami Garden.** Folded-paper robots with crisp creases, washi-patterned belts and paper cranes for checkpoints.

## The pick: 1, Pop-Up Paper Theatre

- **Your robot is the same picture everywhere.** The robot you choose on your phone is the printed cutout standing on the big screen's board. Nobody has to learn which one is theirs.
- **The craft sets the hierarchy.** The floor is a calm printed sheet, and everything that can hurt or help you stands up off it: walls, lasers, push panels, flags and robots. Danger is literally raised.
- **Paper motion explains the rules.** A cutout flips when it turns, tips into a hole cut in the page, springs back up at the reboot token, and gets a hole punched in its ticket at each flag. Every rule is a physical event you can watch.
- **It's warm and authored, not flashy.** It looks like a beautifully printed board game come alive under a lamp, and it needs no glow effects to look good, so it stays smooth on an ordinary laptop.
- **The sound pulls eyes up.** Card flicks, paper slides, a ticket punch and a kalimba melody on every flag are all synthesized and all on the big screen.
